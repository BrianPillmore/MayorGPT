## Governance spine

- Springfield operates under a **council-manager** form of government (fictional Charter, Article II).
- The governing body has **seven members**: five elected by ward and two elected at-large. All serve staggered
  four-year terms.
- The **mayor** is selected by the council from its membership for a two-year term. The mayor presides at meetings,
  signs ordinances and proclamations, and is the ceremonial and intergovernmental representative. The mayor has
  **no veto** and no administrative authority.
- The council and mayor may not direct administrative staff directly; dealings run through the **city manager**
  (Charter, Article IV). Individual members may request information, but requests that need significant staff time
  go through the manager.
- The **city manager** is appointed by and serves at the pleasure of the council, is the chief executive officer,
  prepares and administers the budget, hires and removes department directors, and attends all council meetings.
- The **city clerk** is appointed by the council, keeps the official record, prepares agendas and minutes with the
  manager's office, publishes legal notices, administers municipal elections, and is custodian of the seal.
- The **city attorney** is appointed by the council, serves as legal advisor to the council, manager, and boards,
  and approves contracts and ordinances as to form. The attorney does not advise individual residents.
- Charter-created boards: Planning Commission (seven members) and Board of Zoning Appeals (five members).
  Members are council-appointed.

## Meeting and legislative rhythm

- Regular council meetings are held on the **first and third Tuesdays at 6:30 p.m.** in council chambers.
- Work sessions are held on the **second Tuesday at 5:30 p.m.** and take no formal votes.
- Special meetings may be called by the mayor or any three members with **24 hours' posted notice**.
- Parliamentary authority: **Robert's Rules of Order, Newly Revised**, as modified by council rules of procedure.
- Ordinances require **two readings** at separate meetings and a majority of members present. They take effect
  **30 days** after final passage and publication unless a later date is specified.
- Emergency ordinances require a separate emergency finding and **five affirmative votes**; they expire after
  **90 days** unless re-enacted through the normal process.
- Public hearings on zoning amendments and budget adoption require **15 days' published notice** before the hearing.
- Consent agenda items may be pulled for separate discussion by any one member.

## Fiscal and procurement cues

- The city fiscal year runs **July 1 through June 30**.
- The manager presents a proposed budget by **April 30**; council adopts by **June 15**.
- The charter requires an **independent annual audit**, presented to council within six months of fiscal year end.
- Purchases under **$15,000** may be made by department directors under purchasing policy. Purchases of
  **$15,000 to $74,999** require three written quotes and manager approval. Purchases and contracts of
  **$75,000 or more** require formal sealed bidding or RFP and **council approval**.
- Emergency purchases above the threshold are allowed with manager's written finding and must be reported to
  council at its next regular meeting.
- Council policy sets a general fund unassigned reserve target of **no less than 20 percent** of operating
  expenditures.
- Grant applications over $50,000 or requiring a local match need council authorization before submission.

## Election and transition cues

- Municipal elections are **nonpartisan** and held in **November of odd-numbered years**.
- Candidate filing opens 90 days and closes 60 days before the election; terms begin at the first regular
  meeting in January.
- Members are limited to **three consecutive four-year terms**.
- Recall and citizen initiative are provided in the charter, each requiring signatures from a fixed percentage of
  registered voters (percentages intentionally omitted in this sample).

## Local priorities and context (fictional)

- Adopted strategic plan themes: safe neighborhoods, reliable water infrastructure, a walkable downtown, and
  fiscally sustainable services.
- Standing initiatives: a multi-year water main replacement program, a downtown streetscape project, and a
  comprehensive plan update scheduled for adoption in the next fiscal year.
- Population is roughly 48,000 (fictional). The city operates its own water utility, police, fire, public works,
  parks, and planning departments; library services are provided by a county system.
