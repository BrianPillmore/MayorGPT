# Input: Constituent Escalation — Water Main Break, 1817 Calloway Lane

## Original Resident Letter (received February 28, 2026)

**From:** Patricia Okafor, 1817 Calloway Lane, Weston Falls, TX 76204  
**To:** City of Weston Falls, City Hall  
**Date:** February 28, 2026

---

To Whom It May Concern:

I am writing because I have run out of patience, and I believe the city has run out of excuses.

On February 6th, a water main broke directly in front of my home at 1817 Calloway Lane. Within an hour of the break, a neighbor and I called the city's water emergency line, and a crew was dispatched to shut off the break. I was told a repair would be scheduled within three to five business days.

Eleven days later — on February 17th — the repair was finally completed. During those eleven days:

- The road had an unmarked hole approximately 18 inches across near the center line.
- My water pressure dropped noticeably for the first six days until the city installed a temporary connection.
- I drive a sedan and began using an alternate route, adding approximately four minutes to every trip in or out of my neighborhood.
- I work from home and had a client visit on February 12th — the hole in the road in front of my house was embarrassing.

I called the city's public works line on February 10th and was told, and I am quoting from my own notes, "we're aware of it and it's in the queue." No one offered a timeline. No one from the city reached out proactively to let me know the status.

On February 14th, I submitted a written service request through the city's online portal. I received an auto-acknowledgment. I received nothing else until the repair crew showed up on the 17th.

On March 2nd, I received a form letter from the Utilities Department that said, "Thank you for your patience. The city is committed to maintaining its infrastructure." That letter did not acknowledge the 11-day delay. It did not mention the safety hazard. It did not offer any explanation. It was, frankly, an insult.

I have lived in Weston Falls for 22 years. I have voted in every local election. I pay city taxes and utility fees. I expect better.

I am now posting this on the Weston Falls NextDoor page because I have reason to believe I am not the only resident who has experienced this kind of delay, and I believe the community deserves to know.

Sincerely,  
Patricia Okafor

## Public Works Repair Timeline (internal summary from Director of Public Works, March 5, 2026)

- Feb 6, 7:42 AM: Emergency call received. Crew dispatched 8:15 AM. Break isolated by 9:30 AM.
- Feb 6–7: Assessment confirmed 6-inch cast iron main failure (estimated 1952 installation). Parts for cast iron repair not in stock; procurement initiated Feb 7.
- Feb 7–11: Parts on order. Temporary pressure-maintenance connection installed Feb 9 (explains pressure restoration noted by resident around day 3).
- Feb 11–12: Parts received. Crew scheduling conflict (two crew members out sick; short-staffed week of Feb 10).
- Feb 13–16: Holiday weekend (Presidents' Day, Feb 16). No repair work scheduled on the holiday.
- Feb 17: Repair completed. Road patched. Work order closed.

**Note from Public Works Director:** "The 11-day timeline was longer than our standard and reflects a combination of parts supply and crew availability issues. The road hazard should have been marked within 24 hours of isolation; our records don't show when markings were placed. That is a gap I need to investigate internally."
