## I. What Happened

### Revenue Side

The $1,240,600 projected revenue shortfall has two primary components:

**Sales Tax — $704,000 projected gap (structural)**
Sales tax receipts through January 2026 are running 3.4% below the same period last year, against a budget assumption of +0.5% growth. The decline corresponds to the closure of the Meadowgate Mall anchor tenant in October 2025. Year-over-year comparisons show the decline accelerated from -3.1% in October to -5.4% in December before partially recovering to -5.0% in January. Staff is treating this as a structural problem for the current year; there is no short-term retail recovery expected at the Meadowgate site.

**Franchise Fees — $371,200 projected gap (partially timing)**
Finance staff has identified approximately $180,000 of the franchise fee gap as a timing issue: one cable franchise payment expected by December was received in early February. Adjusting for timing, the structural franchise fee gap is approximately $191,200. The corrected annualized shortfall on franchise fees is approximately $191,200.

**Adjusted Revenue Gap:** After removing the timing item, the revenue shortfall estimate is approximately **$1,060,600** (structural and annualized).

> **Annotation — Timing vs. structural distinction:** This is the single most important analytical discipline in a budget variance memo. The `budget-variance-analysis` skill is designed to force this separation. A memo that adds up all variances and presents a single "shortfall" number without distinguishing timing from structural will mislead council into either panic (if they think a timing gap is permanent) or complacency (if they assume a structural gap will self-correct). The AI correctly flagged the $180K franchise fee item based on the Finance Director's input note. The reviewer must verify this against the actual receivable schedule.

### Expenditure Side

**Police Overtime — $528,000 annualized overrun**
The Police Department is running at 45.1% of budget after five months (41.7% elapsed). The overrun is driven by authorized overtime for two extended criminal investigations and salary backfill for two sworn officer vacancies. The Finance Director estimates the investigation-related overtime at approximately $44,000/month above budget. Staff anticipates the investigations will conclude by May, which would reduce the annualized exposure if the full-year overrun is modeled with a May resolution.

**All other departments:** Spending runs slightly below pace (35.1% of budget at 41.7% elapsed), generating approximately $96,200 in projected savings. This partially offsets the revenue shortfall.

**Net Expenditure Impact:** The police overrun of $528,000 minus $96,200 in other savings = **$431,800 net expenditure pressure.**

## III. Legal and Process Requirements

**Charter Section 7.3 — Budget Amendment Required**
The Police Department overrun is currently 8.1% above the adopted appropriation. Charter Section 7.3 requires council approval by resolution for any mid-year amendment exceeding 5% of any department's adopted appropriation. **This is not optional** — the City Manager cannot administratively authorize continued police spending above the charter threshold. A budget amendment resolution must be adopted before the department's legal spending authority is exhausted.

**Charter deadline:** The budget ordinance requires mid-year amendments to be adopted by April 30. To meet that deadline with proper notice and agenda posting, the amendment resolution should be placed on the April council meeting agenda (assuming March 18 work session provides direction).

**Reserve draw authority:** The City Manager's administrative authority to draw down reserves is governed by the reserve policy and the adopted budget. A drawdown that would bring the reserve below the 15% floor requires council authorization in most jurisdictions. Staff should confirm the city attorney's interpretation of the City Manager's authority before proceeding.

> **Annotation — Legal flags are not optional:** The AI correctly identified two non-discretionary legal constraints: the charter threshold and the amendment deadline. These are not suggestions — they define the decision the council actually has to make. A memo that summarizes the financial situation but omits the legal constraints is worse than no memo, because it can lead a council member to believe the manager can handle everything administratively.

## IV. Options

### Option 1 — Management Response (Partial)
**What it covers:** Implement a spending freeze on non-essential operating expenditures city-wide for the remaining four months (March–June). Estimated savings: $150,000–$200,000. Does not require council action.

**What it does not cover:** Does not resolve the sales tax structural gap or the police charter amendment requirement.

**Recommended for:** Immediate implementation regardless of which broader option is chosen.

### Option 2 — Budget Amendment + Targeted Cuts
**What it covers:** Bring a mid-year budget amendment to council that (a) authorizes additional police appropriation of $528,000 and (b) reduces appropriations in other departments by an equivalent amount. No reserve draw required.

**Tradeoff:** Requires identifying $528,000 in cuts from already-lean budgets. May create service delivery impacts. Requires council buy-in on the cut targets.

### Option 3 — Budget Amendment + Partial Reserve Draw
**What it covers:** Budget amendment authorizes additional police appropriation. Revenue shortfall is covered by a partial reserve draw of approximately $800,000–$1,100,000. Reserve would remain above the 15% floor (barely, in the base case).

**Tradeoff:** Depletes most of the cushion above the reserve floor. Council should understand the reserve position entering FY 2026-27 will be tight. Requires council authorization.

### Option 4 — Structural Revenue Response
**What it covers:** A council discussion of whether the sales tax trend warrants a longer-term revenue response (rate adjustment, new revenue source, service-level adjustment). This is a policy question, not a management question.

**Recommended for:** Work session discussion to gauge appetite for a longer-term response. Not actionable before April.

> **Annotation — Options are layered correctly:** Note that Option 1 is recommended for immediate action regardless of the broader path — this is a management recommendation the Finance Director can make without council direction. Options 2 and 3 require council action and present honest tradeoffs. Option 4 is correctly labeled as a policy question, not an administrative one. A weak draft would collapse all four into a single recommendation or present only one path.

## V. Council Work Session — Decisions Needed

At the March 18 work session, council members need to:

1. **Understand the situation** — revenue shortfall drivers, police overtime cause, reserve position (informational; no action).
2. **Confirm the budget amendment approach** — Option 2 (cuts) or Option 3 (reserve draw) or a combination. Staff needs direction to prepare the amendment resolution.
3. **Authorize or affirm the spending freeze** — Technically a management decision, but council awareness avoids surprises on service impacts.
4. **Discuss appetite for a longer-term revenue conversation** — If they want staff to return with options, this is the time to ask.

**Decisions NOT needed at the work session:** The actual budget amendment vote. That comes at the regular April council meeting after proper notice and agenda posting.

> **Annotation — Separating "inform" from "decide":** The City Manager asked specifically for this separation. The output delivers it. Council members who attend the work session knowing what decisions they are and are not being asked to make will engage more productively. This framing also protects the City Manager from the work session inadvertently becoming an action meeting (open meeting law implications).
