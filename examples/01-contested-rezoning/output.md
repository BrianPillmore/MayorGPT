## II. Background

### Site Description
The subject parcel is 14.2 acres located on the north side of Meridian Bluff Road, approximately 0.3 miles east of the intersection with Halcyon Parkway. The site is currently undeveloped and partially wooded. A drainage easement runs along the east property line. The parcel slopes from the southwest corner toward a low-lying area in the northeast quadrant.

### Surrounding Context
- **North and East:** R-1 Single-Family Residential (Meridian Bluff Estates subdivision, platted 2004)
- **South:** Meridian Bluff Commercial Corridor (C-2 General Commercial); Regional Activity Center boundary follows the south property line
- **West:** C-2 General Commercial; one vacant RM-1 parcel

### Zoning and Land Use History
- Current zoning: R-1, established at annexation in 2003
- **2018:** Prior RM-1 rezoning application on this parcel was withdrawn before hearing
- **2021:** Council denied a Future Land Use Map amendment to reclassify this parcel from Suburban Residential to Medium-Density Residential (4-1 vote)

> **Annotation — History matters:** The AI surfaced the 2021 FLUM denial and the 2018 withdrawn application from the input materials. ## III. Comprehensive Plan Consistency Analysis

### Favorable Indicators
**Policy H-4** (Housing Element) calls for encouraging medium- and high-density residential development within one-quarter mile of Regional Activity Centers. The south portion of the subject parcel is within one-quarter mile of the Meridian Bluff Regional Activity Center.

### Unfavorable Indicators — Material Inconsistency
**Figure LU-3 (Future Land Use Map)** designates the subject parcel as "Suburban Residential," not "Medium-Density Residential." The Regional Activity Center boundary runs along the *south edge* of the parcel. The northern two-thirds of the site is designated Suburban Residential, which is consistent with the surrounding R-1 zoning pattern.

**Policy LU-7** (Land Use Element) requires transitions from higher-intensity to lower-intensity uses to be achieved through graduated density buffers or physical separating features. No graduated buffer district exists on this parcel or between the R-1 neighborhood to the north and east and the proposed RM-2 zoning.

### Staff Assessment
There is a material tension between Policy H-4 (which supports the general concept) and Figure LU-3 (which designates the site Suburban Residential) and the 2021 FLUM denial (which specifically declined to reclassify this parcel). A rezoning to RM-2 would be inconsistent with the adopted Future Land Use Map unless the Map is first amended or the commission makes specific findings that the policy text supersedes the map designation. Staff does not consider that finding supportable on the current record.

> **Annotation — Plan consistency is the first criterion:** The AI did exactly what a competent planning director would do: it ran the criteria in order and identified the most significant legal vulnerability first. The FLUM designation inconsistency and the 2021 denial are the two facts most likely to make a rezoning approval legally contestable. This is not the AI being anti-development — it is the AI being legally disciplined. A reviewer can disagree with the conclusion, but the analysis is correctly framed.

## IV. Criteria Analysis (Municipal Code Section 4.12.030)

### Criterion (a): Consistency with the Comprehensive Plan
**Status: Not met on current record.** See Section III above. The Future Land Use Map designation is Suburban Residential; the 2021 FLUM amendment was denied. Policy H-4 provides partial support for the southern portion of the parcel but does not resolve the FLUM inconsistency for the site as a whole.

### Criterion (b): Compatibility with Surrounding Land Uses
**Status: Partially met, with concerns.** The south boundary of the parcel adjoins commercial uses, which are generally compatible with medium-density residential. However, the north and east sides directly abut R-1 lots in Meridian Bluff Estates. The applicant proposes a 30-foot setback and landscaped buffers along these boundaries. Whether a 30-foot setback from an 18 du/ac development is adequate to satisfy the graduated transition requirement in Policy LU-7 requires Planning Commission judgment. Staff notes the buffer width does not meet the informal standard applied in prior RM-2 approvals in the city (minimum 50 feet or a dedicated open space tract).

### Criterion (c): Infrastructure Capacity
**Status: Cannot be assessed — studies incomplete.** Three required analyses have not been finalized:
- Hydraulic model confirming water fire flow at RM-2 density (expected April 3)
- Sewer peak factor analysis (Public Works preliminary review indicates the 8-inch main at 74% capacity may be stressed by an additional ~28,665 gpd; confirmation pending)
- Traffic Impact Analysis (submitted March 3; Public Works review in progress)

Staff cannot make a finding on infrastructure adequacy until these studies are completed and reviewed. This is the primary basis for the continuation recommendation.

### Criterion (d): Spot Zoning
**Status: Significant concern.** No RM-2 parcel exists within 1,200 feet of the subject parcel on three sides. The site is surrounded on the north, east, and partial west by R-1 uses. The applicant's argument that proximity to the Regional Activity Center establishes rational basis may be sufficient if the FLUM issue is resolved, but the spot zoning concern cannot be dismissed without a clear findings trail. The city attorney should advise on the legal standard for spot zoning under state law and whether a 4-1 prior FLUM denial heightens the burden.

> **Annotation — Criterion-by-criterion discipline:** This is what the `zoning-case-analysis` skill is designed to produce: a structured walk through each adoptable criterion with a clear status call. Note that the AI flagged criteria (c) as "cannot be assessed" rather than guessing. This is correct and important. A draft that guessed at infrastructure adequacy would expose the city to a meritless approval. The reviewer's job is to confirm the status calls and add any local knowledge the AI didn't have access to.
