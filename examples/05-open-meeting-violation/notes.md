# Notes: Open Meetings Law Violation Example

## What made this output strong

1. **The issue statement was precise.** It named the legal doctrine (walking quorum), the alleged conduct, and the two legal theories in one paragraph. A vague issue statement produces a vague analysis. Starting with a clean, specific issue statement is a discipline the city attorney should require of every legal risk memo, AI-assisted or not.

2. **The elements table applied facts to law.** The four-element walking quorum table — serial contacts, quorum-touching, public business, deliberation — is not generic legal education. It is applied analysis with a risk assessment for each element. The "key unknown" identification (content of the communications) is the most important output: it tells the city attorney what factual step must come before the legal analysis can be completed.

3. **The criminal-vs-civil distinction was made explicitly.** Officials and council members often conflate "criminal" with "violation." Making this distinction clearly helps the council understand the full risk spectrum without either minimizing or catastrophizing the situation.

4. **The charter provision was identified as a separate basis.** The city attorney's reflex is often to analyze only the state statute. The AI correctly identified that the charter provides an independent basis for a finding of impropriety, and also that the failure to adopt a communications policy is itself a separate governance problem.

5. **The clerk's checklist had clear boundaries.** The list of what the clerk does NOT do is as important as the list of what she does. Role confusion in a sensitive political situation can create additional problems faster than the underlying complaint.

## Common mistakes to avoid

- **Do not share Part A with the full council before the city attorney has reviewed it.** This is an attorney-client privileged working document. If it is distributed broadly, the privilege may be waived.
- **Do not notify the complainant about the investigation's progress in substance.** The acknowledgment letter is appropriate; substantive updates should be routed through the attorney. The complainant has indicated he may go to media — every communication with him is potentially public.
- **Do not let the council vote on the contract or any related matter before the legal review is complete.** Re-voting before the legal question is resolved could compound the problem.
- **Do not use this output as a substitute for state-law research.** The walking quorum doctrine varies by state — some states have specific AG opinions that define the elements more or less restrictively than the general framework above. The city attorney must conduct independent jurisdiction-specific research.

## Variations

### When the complaint involves a text message chain
Serial text messaging creates the same walking quorum risk as phone calls and emails, but presents additional practical problems: the messages may be on personal devices, the records may be subject to a public records request, and text-based communications are easy to screenshot and share publicly. The same analytical framework applies; add a note about device discovery.

### When the complaint is about a city board or commission (not the council)
Open meetings law applies to city boards and commissions with governmental authority, not just the council. The same combination applies — use the city attorney agent for legal analysis and the clerk agent for records/process, but note that the board/commission likely has a different quorum calculation.

### When the city wants to prevent future violations
After the complaint is resolved, use:
- `skills/people/ai-acceptable-use-policy/` (if the issue was also about digital communications)
- `skills/governance/open-meeting-risk-check/` in training mode — to help council members understand the doctrine before future deliberative situations
- A new agenda item to adopt the charter-required communications policy

### For a less serious situation (no formal complaint, just a close call)
If a council member is about to engage in pre-meeting serial communications and wants to check whether it is appropriate, use the `open-meeting-risk-check` skill preventively. Run the scenario (without personally identifying information) and get a risk flag before the communications happen. Prevention is far less costly than remediation.
