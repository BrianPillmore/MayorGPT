"Confirmed" means verified by a direct official source. "Reported" means received but not yet independently verified. "Unverified" means received from one source, not confirmed. An AI brief that does not enforce this distinction is dangerous — it gives decision-makers false confidence about the certainty of the information they are acting on.

> **Annotation — The message house is the boundary:** The crisis message house is a discipline tool, not a script. The mayor reads from prepared remarks but can answer questions. The message house tells her what she can say, what she cannot say, and how to handle the hardest questions. If the PIO and mayor are not aligned on the message house before the statement, they will say different things to different reporters.

---

## Holding Statement (for use if media interaction before 10:30 PM)

"Tonight's tornado caused significant damage in northeast Millvale. Our emergency teams are on the ground right now focused on life safety. I will have a full statement for you at 10:30."

> **Annotation — Holding statement is short for a reason:** The holding statement buys time without committing to facts that may change. It says "significant damage," not "X homes destroyed." It says "life safety" as the priority, which is true. It sets a specific time for the full statement, which manages media expectations without running from them. Do not expand this statement before the 10:30 PM mark.

## Key Messages (full statement, approximately 10:30 PM)

**Open with:**
> "At 6:47 this evening, a tornado moved through northeast Millvale. Our emergency teams responded immediately, and those teams are still working right now. Our first priority tonight is making sure every resident who needs help has it."

**On injuries:**
> "We have confirmed that three residents were transported to Regional Medical Center with injuries. I am not prepared to characterize the severity of those injuries at this time. Our search of the affected areas is still ongoing and I want to be honest with you: we will give you complete information as soon as we have it, and not before."

**On shelter:**
> "The Red Cross has opened an emergency shelter at the Millvale Community Center on Oak Avenue. Anyone who cannot safely return home tonight should go there."

**On power:**
> "Midcoast Electric is working to restore power. Their current estimate is 24 to 72 hours for the affected area. I know that is a wide range; we will update that estimate as work progresses."

**On school:**
> "Northgate Elementary sustained damage to the gymnasium wing. The building was empty at the time. We are inspecting the building now and will communicate with families about school operations as soon as we have completed that inspection — I expect to have that information by tomorrow morning."

**On next steps:**
> "I will hold a public briefing at Millvale Community Center tomorrow at 10 AM with a full damage assessment. I am asking residents in the Thornwood Estates and Millvale Heights areas to stay out of any structure that has been marked with a red or yellow tag until it has been cleared."

> **Annotation — "I am not prepared to characterize" phrasing:** This is the right language when injury severity or casualty count is uncertain. It is honest — the mayor genuinely is not prepared to characterize it — and it signals transparency rather than evasion. The alternative ("we have no fatalities at this time") is dangerous because it may change in the next hour. Never use "at this time" on a fatality statement — it implies you expect that to change.

> **Annotation — School parents get a specific commitment:** Parents of Northgate students are a specific, anxious audience. Giving them a specific time ("tomorrow morning") for school-status information prevents a night of anxiety and a flood of calls to city hall. The commitment is realistic (a fire marshal inspection by morning is achievable). Do not make this commitment unless you have confirmed with the fire marshal that the inspection will actually happen by morning.

## Tough Q&A

**Q: How many people were killed?**
A: "I have no confirmed fatalities. Our search of the damaged areas is still ongoing and I will give you accurate information as we have it."

**Q: Can you tell us what happened to [specific address or person]?**
A: "I'm not going to discuss individual residents or properties at this stage — that will come through official notifications to families first."

**Q: Is the city prepared for this? Were there enough warnings?**
A: "Tonight is about response. I'm going to focus on helping residents right now; we will do a thorough after-action review once this immediate response is complete."

**Q: How much damage is there in dollar terms?**
A: "It is far too early for a dollar estimate and I won't give you a number that isn't accurate. We will have a preliminary damage assessment at tomorrow's 10 AM briefing."

**Q: Will there be a federal disaster declaration?**
A: "That process starts with a state assessment. We are in contact with state emergency management. I'm focused on local response right now."

> **Annotation — Deflecting the damage dollar question:** Journalists always ask for a dollar number in the first hours. The correct answer is always "too early." Cities that give early damage estimates routinely get it wrong in both directions, and the number becomes the story rather than the response. "We will have a preliminary assessment at tomorrow's briefing" gives the reporter a specific future anchor.

> **Annotation — "Tonight is about response" on the preparedness question:** This deflects a potentially adversarial question without being evasive. It is honest (tonight IS about response) and it sets the expectation that a real answer will come (the after-action review). A mayor who gets defensive about the preparedness question at hour three of an active incident looks worse than one who defers it calmly.

## What NOT to say tonight

- Do not give injury severity descriptions.
- Do not mention specific addresses with structural collapses (next-of-kin notification first).
- Do not name individual injured residents.
- Do not speculate on tornado path, intensity, or cause.
- Do not estimate dollar damage.
