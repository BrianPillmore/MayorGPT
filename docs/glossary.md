# Glossary of Municipal Terms

Plain-language definitions of terms used throughout MayorGPT. Definitions are general; state and local law often changes details. Where a term varies, the entry says so. This is not legal advice.

## Governance and process
- **Agenda packet**: The compiled materials (agenda, staff reports, attachments) given to a governing body before a meeting.
- **Charter**: A city's local constitution, where adopted. Sets structure, powers, and procedures.
- **Consent agenda**: A group of routine items approved with one motion, unless pulled for separate discussion.
- **Council-manager form**: A form of government in which an elected council hires a professional manager to run daily operations.
- **Strong-mayor form**: A form in which the mayor holds substantial executive authority.
- **Ex parte contact**: Communication with a decision-maker outside the formal record in a quasi-judicial matter; restrictions vary by state.
- **Executive session / closed session**: A meeting closed to the public for narrow, legally defined topics. Allowed topics and procedure vary by state.
- **Open meetings law**: State law requiring public notice, access, and records for governing body meetings. Names and details vary by state.
- **Public records law**: State law giving the public access to government records, with exemptions. Names and details vary by state.
- **Quorum**: The minimum number of members needed to conduct business.
- **Resolution**: A formal expression of the governing body's decision or policy, usually less formal than an ordinance.
- **Ordinance**: A local law adopted by the governing body, typically with readings and notice.
- **Home rule**: The authority of a city to govern itself in local matters, subject to state law. Scope varies widely.
- **Dillon's Rule**: A legal approach limiting cities to powers expressly granted by the state; applies in some states.
- **Interlocal agreement**: An agreement between governments to share services or costs.
- **Proclamation**: A ceremonial or emergency declaration issued by the mayor or governing body.
- **Quasi-judicial**: A decision applying existing criteria to specific facts (for example, a variance), requiring due process and a record. Compare **legislative**, which sets general policy.
- **Staff report**: The written analysis and recommendation prepared by staff for a decision-making body.

## Finance
- **General Fund**: The main operating fund for general government services.
- **Enterprise fund**: A fund for self-supporting services such as water or sewer, funded by user fees.
- **Fund balance**: The difference between assets and liabilities in a governmental fund; policy often sets a minimum.
- **Structural deficit**: Recurring expenses exceed recurring revenue even in a normal year.
- **One-time revenue**: Revenue not expected to recur, which should not fund recurring costs.
- **CIP (Capital Improvement Plan)**: A multi-year plan for major capital projects and their funding.
- **Appropriation**: Legal authority to spend a specified amount for a purpose.
- **Budget amendment**: A formal change to an adopted budget.
- **Mill levy / millage / tax rate**: A property tax rate expressed in terms of assessed value; terminology varies by state.
- **Assessed value**: The value used to compute property taxes, often a fraction of market value.
- **TIF (Tax Increment Financing)**: A tool that captures future increases in property tax revenue within a district to pay for improvements; rules vary by state.
- **Special assessment**: A charge on benefitting properties for a specific improvement.
- **GFOA**: Government Finance Officers Association, which publishes best practices.
- **ACFR**: Annual Comprehensive Financial Report, the audited annual financial statement.
- **Single audit**: A required audit when a government spends federal awards above a threshold set by federal rules.
- **Bond covenant**: A promise to bondholders, such as maintaining rates sufficient to cover debt service.
- **Debt service coverage**: Net revenue divided by annual debt service.
- **User fee / cost of service**: Fees designed to recover the cost of providing a service.

## Land use and development
- **Comprehensive plan**: A long-range policy plan for growth and land use.
- **Zoning**: Regulations governing land use, density, and form by district.
- **Rezoning**: A change to a parcel's zoning district.
- **Variance**: Permission to depart from a zoning standard because of a hardship defined in local law.
- **Conditional use / special use permit**: Permission for a use allowed only under specified conditions.
- **Subdivision**: Division of land into lots, regulated by local standards.
- **Development agreement**: A contract between a city and developer setting obligations and sometimes vesting rights; legal availability and limits vary by state.
- **Vested rights**: Protection of a developer from later changes to regulations; scope varies by state.
- **Impact fee**: A fee on new development to fund related infrastructure; limits vary by state.
- **Annexation**: Adding territory to a city.
- **Spot zoning**: A rezoning of a small area that is inconsistent with the plan and primarily benefits a private party; treatment varies by state.
- **Findings of fact**: Written conclusions tied to evidence in the record that support a decision.
- **Level of service (LOS)**: A measure of how well infrastructure (such as a road) performs.
- **Right-of-way**: Land set aside for public use such as streets and utilities.

## Procurement and grants
- **RFP**: Request for proposals, used when quality and price are both considered.
- **RFQ / ITB**: Request for quotes / invitation to bid, typically price-driven.
- **Sole source**: Purchase from one vendor without competition, allowed only under defined justifications.
- **Cooperative purchasing**: Using a contract competed by another government.
- **NOFA / NOFO**: Notice of funding availability / opportunity for a grant.
- **Match (cost share)**: Local funds a grantee must contribute.
- **Pass-through**: Funds that flow from a federal or state agency through another government to the city.
- **Subrecipient**: An entity that receives a grant through another recipient.
- **Reimbursement grant**: A grant paid after the grantee has spent money.
- **Uniform Guidance (2 CFR 200)**: Federal rules governing federal award administration; applicability depends on the award.

## Public safety and emergency
- **EOC**: Emergency Operations Center.
- **ICS / NIMS**: Incident Command System / National Incident Management System.
- **Mutual aid**: Agreements for agencies to assist each other in emergencies.
- **Local emergency declaration**: A formal declaration enabling emergency powers; process set by state and local law.
- **After-action report**: A review of what happened and what to improve after an incident.
- **CJIS**: Criminal Justice Information Services, an FBI division and security policy governing certain criminal justice data.

## People and HR
- **Collective bargaining**: Negotiation between an employer and a union; scope and process vary by state.
- **Mandatory subject of bargaining**: A topic the employer must bargain over; defined by state law.
- **Due process (public employment)**: Procedural protections before certain adverse actions.
- **Weingarten rights**: A right to representation in certain investigatory interviews, recognized in private-sector law and in some public-sector jurisdictions; applicability varies.
- **At-will employment**: Employment that may end at any time for lawful reasons; many public employees have additional protections.
- **Reasonable accommodation**: An adjustment for disability or religion required by law unless it causes undue hardship.

## Communications and accessibility
- **PIO**: Public Information Officer.
- **ADA**: Americans with Disabilities Act.
- **Section 508**: Federal accessibility requirements for electronic information technology, which many agencies adopt as a standard.
- **WCAG**: Web Content Accessibility Guidelines.
- **Plain language**: Writing that readers can understand the first time they read it.
- **LEP**: Limited English proficiency.
- **Language access plan**: A plan for providing services to LEP residents.

## AI governance
- **Hallucination**: AI-generated content that sounds plausible but is false.
- **Human-in-the-loop**: A person reviews and approves outputs before use.
- **Acceptable use policy**: Rules for how staff may use a tool.
- **Accountability trace**: A record of who reviewed what in a multi-agent workflow (see `../stacks/ORCHESTRATION_PROTOCOL.md`).
- **PII**: Personally identifiable information.
