# Municipal Output Rubric

Score each criterion from 1 (weak) to 5 (strong).

## Criteria
1. **Role alignment**: Did the draft stay within the right municipal role boundary?
2. **Process awareness**: Did it surface notice, hearing, procurement, records, election, or HR process issues where relevant?
3. **Factual discipline**: Did it distinguish facts, assumptions, and unknowns?
4. **Fiscal realism**: Did it identify costs, funding sources, or recurring impacts where relevant?
5. **Legal and risk awareness**: Did it flag obvious legal or risk issues without pretending to replace counsel?
6. **Clarity**: Could the target audience understand and use it?
7. **Actionability**: Did it end with next steps, owners, or review gates?
8. **Role boundary adherence**: Did the output stay within the scope of authority belonging to the role it was drafted from? A Finance Director draft should not make policy recommendations reserved for Council. A City Attorney draft should not commit the city to a legal position without noting it requires formal opinion. A department head draft should not authorize spending above their delegation threshold. Score low if the output implies authority the role does not have or omits a mandatory referral to a higher authority.
9. **Source discipline**: Does the output clearly distinguish what came from attached source documents, what reflects general municipal knowledge, and what is inference or synthesis? Strong outputs label the basis for each significant claim ("per the attached audit report," "based on general GFOA practice," "this is inferred from the trend — confirm with Finance"). Weak outputs blend sources without attribution, making it impossible for the human reviewer to verify claims or identify where AI filled gaps.

## Pass threshold
A draft is usually ready for human editing when:
- no category is below 3, and
- at least five categories score 4 or 5.
