# HR and Personnel Rubric

Use this rubric for scoring hiring materials, disciplinary memos, investigation intake documents, and performance reviews. Score each dimension from 1 (weak) to 5 (strong).

## Dimensions

1. **Due process discipline**: Did the output respect the procedural requirements that apply to the employee's classification and the type of action being taken? A strong output identifies the applicable process (at-will notice, civil service hearing rights, pre-disciplinary Skelly notice, Garrity warning for compelled statements, or collective bargaining grievance procedure) and confirms that the document is being produced at the right stage in that process. A weak output proceeds as if all employees have the same rights, or omits process gates that are required before the action can be lawfully taken.

2. **Employment law compliance**: Did the output use only job-related, documented, and behavioral criteria? Federal and state employment law prohibits adverse employment actions based on protected characteristics. A strong output grounds every criterion, question, and disciplinary finding in specific job duties, documented conduct, or measurable performance. A weak output uses vague subjective language ("not a fit," "lacks energy," "difficult to work with") that is legally indefensible and easy to challenge. Every hiring question, performance standard, and disciplinary finding must be traceable to a specific job requirement or documented incident.

3. **Factual vs. conclusory distinction**: Did the output clearly separate documented facts from characterizations, inferences, and conclusions? In disciplinary and investigation documents, this distinction is legally critical. A strong output states: "On [date], [employee] was observed doing X, as documented in [source]." A weak output states: "The employee repeatedly exhibited dishonest behavior." Every conclusion must be traceable to a specific, dated, documented fact. AI has a tendency toward conclusory framing; this dimension requires aggressive human editing.

4. **Privacy protection**: Did the output avoid including, summarizing, or referencing information that should not appear in the document being drafted — including medical records, EAP participation, accommodation requests, unrelated prior discipline, third-party personal information, or any detail not necessary for the specific action being taken? A strong output contains only the information relevant to the matter at hand. A weak output incorporates tangential personal information that creates privacy exposure, litigation risk, or chilling effects on employees exercising protected rights.

5. **Legal-review flags**: Did the output identify the points at which City Attorney or outside employment counsel must review before the document is used? A strong output flags required review gates explicitly: "City Attorney review required before this letter is issued," "confirm Skelly hearing procedures with HR Director and City Attorney," "this investigation report should not be shared with the employee until counsel has reviewed." A weak output produces polished documents without noting where legal review is mandatory, creating risk that a legally deficient document gets used.

## Pass threshold
An HR or personnel output is ready for HR Director review when:
- Due process discipline scores 4 or 5,
- Employment law compliance scores 4 or 5,
- Factual vs. conclusory distinction scores 3 or higher,
- no dimension scores below 3, and
- Legal-review flags scores 4 or 5.

All AI-generated HR and personnel documents must be reviewed by the HR Director before use, and any document related to discipline, investigation, or separation must also be reviewed by City Attorney before it is issued or shared with an employee. This rubric evaluates whether the AI output is a safe starting point for those reviews — not whether it is ready to use.
