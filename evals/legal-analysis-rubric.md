# Legal Analysis Rubric

Use this rubric for scoring legal memos, ordinance drafts, contract reviews, and written findings. Score each dimension from 1 (weak) to 5 (strong).

## Dimensions

1. **Issue identification**: Did the output correctly identify the central legal question(s) and any subsidiary issues? A strong output names the issue precisely (e.g., "whether the proposed fee is a tax requiring a vote under Proposition 218" rather than "whether the fee is legal"). A weak output misframes the issue, misses a key subsidiary question, or addresses a question that was not actually asked.

2. **Legal authority grounding**: Did the output cite specific legal authority — statutes, ordinances, case law, regulations, or attorney general opinions — for each material proposition? A strong output quotes or paraphrases the controlling text and identifies the jurisdiction. A weak output asserts legal conclusions without authority, or cites authority that does not actually support the proposition.

3. **Risk calibration**: Did the output characterize legal risk accurately and proportionately? A strong output distinguishes between clear prohibitions, gray areas, and low-risk interpretive questions, and explains what factors drive the risk level. A weak output either minimizes real risk ("this should be fine") or overstates risk on settled questions, making the output unhelpful for decision-making.

4. **Hedging appropriateness**: Did the output hedge where appropriate without hedging everything into uselessness? Legal analysis produced by AI is not a formal legal opinion. A strong output identifies where formal City Attorney opinion is required, where the analysis is preliminary, and where the uncertainty is factual rather than legal. A weak output either presents AI analysis as definitive legal advice or hedges so pervasively that no actionable guidance survives.

5. **Review-gate specificity**: Did the output clearly identify what must happen before the city can act — and who must do it? A strong output names specific review gates: "City Attorney must issue a formal written opinion before this ordinance is introduced," "the contract must be reviewed by bond counsel before execution," "Council must make specific findings at the public hearing." A weak output uses vague language like "legal review is recommended" without specifying the type of review, the reviewer, or the timing.

## Pass threshold
A legal analysis output is ready for attorney review when:
- Issue identification scores 4 or 5,
- Legal authority grounding scores 3 or higher,
- no dimension scores below 3, and
- Review-gate specificity scores 4 or 5.

Legal analysis output produced by AI should never be treated as a substitute for City Attorney review. This rubric evaluates whether the AI output is a useful starting point for that review, not whether it is ready for action.
