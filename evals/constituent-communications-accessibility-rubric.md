# Constituent Communications and Accessibility Rubric

Use this for resident letters, notices, FAQs, web content, emergency alerts, social posts, and meeting notices. Complements `public-facing-writing-rubric.md`, which covers tone and defensibility; this rubric covers whether residents of all abilities and languages can actually use the communication.

Score each criterion from 1 (weak) to 5 (strong).

## Criteria
1. **Plain language**: Short sentences, common words, active voice, and the key action first. Jargon and acronyms are defined or removed. Reading level fits the audience (many public guidelines suggest roughly an 8th-grade level or lower for general notices; use your city's standard).
2. **Structure for scanning**: Descriptive headings, short paragraphs, lists for steps, and what/when/where/how to respond visible at the top.
3. **Accessible format readiness**: Text is suitable for screen readers: real headings and lists, descriptive link text (not "click here"), no information conveyed by color alone, alt text for images and charts, captions or transcripts for audio and video, and accessible tables. Documents are expected to be tagged and tested before posting.
4. **Section 508 and ADA awareness**: Does the output flag the need to meet the accessibility standard your city is required to follow (for example, WCAG-based requirements under applicable federal or state rules) and that final files must be tested by a human or tool? It must not claim a document "is compliant" on its own say-so. Requirements vary by jurisdiction and funding source; confirm with the City Attorney or ADA coordinator.
5. **Alternate formats and contact paths**: Offers phone, TTY/relay, large print, and other accommodations, and tells people how to request them. Offers more than one channel for residents without internet.
6. **Language access**: Identifies likely translation needs from local demographic data, uses qualified human review for translation of important notices, avoids idioms that translate poorly, and does not post unreviewed machine translation as an official version.
7. **Cultural and situational sensitivity**: Avoids assumptions about household, status, ability, or literacy; does not require residents to share immigration status or other sensitive data unless legally required.
8. **Accuracy and status labeling**: Facts are limited to what is confirmed; estimates and unknowns are labeled; next-update time is stated for fast-moving matters.
9. **Action clarity**: Residents know exactly what to do, by when, and who to contact. Deadlines and rights (comment, appeal, request) are stated accurately from the source.
10. **Privacy**: No personal information of residents or employees beyond what is necessary and approved.

## Automatic fail conditions
- Claims legal accessibility compliance without noting verification is needed.
- Publishes machine translation as an official translation without human review noted.
- Conveys a critical deadline or safety instruction only in an inaccessible format (image-only, color-only, audio-only).
- Includes unredacted personal information.

## Pass threshold
- No criterion below 3, no automatic fail condition, and
- criteria 3, 6, 8, and 9 score 4 or 5 for any emergency, health, or deadline-driven notice.

A passing score clears the draft for final accessibility testing and translation review, not for posting.
