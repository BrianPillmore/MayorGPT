# Public Engagement

Skills for constituent responses, messaging, speeches, public meetings, FAQs, and communication.

| Skill | What it does |
|---|---|
| `constituent-response` | Draft a respectful response to a resident issue while routing the matter internally and protecting the city from overpromising. Templates: `response-template.md`. |
| `crisis-message-house` | Build a disciplined crisis message house with holding statement, key messages, and tough Q&A. Templates: `holding-statement-template.md`. |
| `engagement-plan-designer` | Design a proportionate public engagement plan for a city decision, matching methods to the decision, audience, legal notice duties, and timeline. |
| `faq-builder` | Convert a project, policy, or controversy into a plain-language public FAQ that reduces repeated confusion. |
| `language-access-plan` | Build a practical language-access and accessibility plan for a notice, hearing, program, or outreach campaign serving limited-English-proficient and disabled residents. |
| `meeting-notice-drafter` | Draft a legally sufficient public notice for any city council, board, commission, or committee meeting — covering required content, posting deadlines, and special-meeting or emergency-meeting rules. Templates: `meeting-notice-template.md`. |
| `press-release-writer` | Write a city press release that is accurate, readable, and aligned with legal and operational review needs. Templates: `release-template.md`. |
| `public-comment-summarizer` | Synthesize hearing or meeting comments into themes, misconceptions, unanswered questions, and response options. |
| `social-post-series` | Generate a multi-post city social media series with tone control, accessibility, and channel fit. |
| `speechwriter` | Draft remarks for the mayor, council, manager, or department head in a strong but grounded municipal voice. |
| `town-hall-kit` | Prepare a town hall kit with agenda, ground rules, likely questions, follow-up method, and message priorities. |

**Notes:** These skills provide issue-spotting and drafting frameworks, not legal advice. State and local law varies; always verify deadlines, thresholds, and procedures against your own charter, code, and state statutes, and route flagged items to the city attorney.
