# Town Hall Kit

**Purpose:** Plan a town hall that is orderly, inclusive, legally safe, and produces usable input. Decide first whether it is a **noticed public meeting** of a body or an informational event; this affects open-meetings obligations.

## Required inputs
- Topic, goals (inform, hear, decide), and decisions still open
- Hosts and attendees; whether a quorum of any public body will attend (if so, meeting-law notice and no-deliberation rules may apply; see `open-meeting-risk-check`)
- Venue, date, accessibility, language needs, budget, security plan
- Staff and subject experts; moderator

## Workflow
1. **Purpose and format.** Choose structure: presentation plus Q&A, stations/open house, small-group tables, or listening session. Q&A-only formats get dominated by the loudest voices; add written cards, tables, or online submission.
2. **Notice and outreach:** announce at least two weeks ahead through multiple channels (mail, social, schools, community groups, language media); notice requirements if a quorum attends (see `meeting-notice-drafter`).
3. **Logistics:** ADA access, interpretation and translation, childcare, transit, time of day, livestream or recording, closed captions, microphones, signage.
4. **Agenda (90-120 min):** welcome and ground rules (5), context and facts (15), what is decided and when (5), structured input (45-60), summary and next steps (10).
5. **Ground rules:** equal time per speaker, one question at a time, respect for people, no signs blocking views, what happens with disruptions. Rules apply to everyone and avoid viewpoint judgments (see `first-amendment-comment-limits`).
6. **Message priorities:** three messages, facts sheet, and what the city can and cannot commit to at the event.
7. **Q&A prep:** top 15 likely and hardest questions with approved answers, owner for each, and "park" process for unknowns. Track unanswered questions publicly.
8. **Capture input:** note-takers, comment cards, sticky-dot boards, online form; capture demographics optionally to see who was heard.
9. **Security and safety:** coordination with police, de-escalation plan, emergency exits, and handling of threats.
10. **Follow-up:** thank-you message, summary of what was heard (see `public-comment-summarizer`), answers to parked questions within a stated timeframe, and next steps.

## Output structure
- Run-of-show with timings and roles
- Ground rules script and moderator cues
- Q&A bank and fact sheet
- Logistics and accessibility checklist
- Follow-up plan with owners and dates

## Red flags / escalate to the city attorney
- A council quorum attending without notice
- Discussion of pending litigation, personnel, or quasi-judicial cases
- Threats, protest, or removal of a speaker
- Candidate or ballot-measure advocacy

## Common mistakes
- Using the event to announce decisions not yet made
- Letting officials deliberate with each other
- No visible follow-up, which erodes trust

*Not legal advice.*
