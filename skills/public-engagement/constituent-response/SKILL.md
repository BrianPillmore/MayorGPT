# Constituent Response

**Purpose:** Answer a resident's message clearly and humanely, route the issue to the right owner, and avoid commitments the city cannot keep. Use `response-template.md`.

## Required inputs
- The message (verbatim) and channel; sender's name and contact, if provided
- What the city knows: prior contacts, open service requests, relevant policy or code
- Responsible department and realistic timeline
- Sensitivity: threats, safety hazard, legal claim, personnel matter, candidate/campaign context

## Workflow
1. **Triage urgency.** Emergency or safety hazard: direct to 911 or the right hotline and notify the department at once. Threats or self-harm language: follow the city protocol.
2. **Classify the request:** service request, information question, complaint about staff, policy opinion, legal claim or demand, records request (route to the records officer; the clock may be running), or media inquiry.
3. **Find the facts.** Check the service-request system before replying. Do not guess status.
4. **Write the response:** acknowledge the specific concern in the resident's own terms; state what the city can and cannot do and why, in plain language; name the next step, owner, and realistic timeframe; give one contact point. Avoid blame, jargon, and reference to unrelated matters.
5. **Do not promise** outcomes, funding, enforcement against a neighbor, or council votes. Elected officials should not direct administrative decisions outside the charter's channels.
6. **Handle special cases.** Legal threats or claims: do not argue; acknowledge receipt and route to the attorney or risk manager. Personnel complaints: route per HR policy and do not discuss other employees. Land-use matters before a body: do not discuss the merits if the member sits in a quasi-judicial role; forward the message to the clerk for the record (see `ethics-conflict-check`, `public-hearing-prep`).
7. **Privacy and records:** constituent emails are generally public records; do not include other residents' personal data, and do not promise confidentiality.
8. **Log and follow up:** record the response and set a reminder to confirm resolution.

## Output structure
1. Triage note (internal): category, urgency, owner, deadline
2. Draft reply (short: 120-200 words)
3. Internal routing message to the department
4. Follow-up date

## Example
> "Thank you for letting us know about the broken streetlight at Oak and 5th. I have reported it to Public Works (request #1234). Crews generally inspect within 5 business days, and I will confirm once it is scheduled. You can check status at ___ or call ___."

## Red flags / escalate to the city attorney
- Claims, demands, or notices of intent to sue; requests under public-records law
- Allegations of discrimination, harassment, or misconduct by staff
- A request to take or avoid action for personal benefit by an official

## Common mistakes
- Replying before verifying the status
- Over-apologizing in a way that admits liability
- Copying large distribution lists (also serial-meeting risk for officials)

*Not legal advice.*
