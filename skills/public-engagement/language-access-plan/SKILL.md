# Language Access Plan

**Purpose:** Make sure residents can understand and take part regardless of language, disability, or literacy. Legal duties come from federal civil-rights law (Title VI for federal-fund recipients), the ADA and Section 504, plus state language laws and city policy; **verify what applies, and have the attorney confirm obligations.**

## Required inputs
- The program, notice, or event and its audience; deadlines
- Local language data (Census/ACS, school district, 311 and court interpreter requests) and community partners
- Existing city language-access policy, vendor contracts, and translation glossary
- Budget and turnaround time

## Workflow
1. **Identify languages** using the four-factor approach commonly used in federal guidance: number or proportion of LEP residents, frequency of contact, importance of the service, and available resources. Include sign language and text-only needs.
2. **Classify materials:** vital documents (notices of rights, hearing notices, benefit and enforcement notices, emergency alerts) must be translated first; others as resources allow. Decide interpretation (oral) vs. translation (written) for each.
3. **Quality:** professional or qualified translators and interpreters, not family members, children, or machine output alone. Use machine translation only for drafts with human review; keep glossaries (planning, public-works terms). Back-translation for critical documents.
4. **Process:** build the notice ("language assistance available at ___" in top languages), request-by-date method that is not burdensome, and standing interpretation capacity at hearings. Allow longer comment time for interpreted speakers if your rules permit.
5. **Disability access:** accessible venues, captions, screen-reader-friendly documents (tagged PDFs), alt text, plain language, large print, assistive listening, ASL, remote options, and reasonable accommodation contacts.
6. **Outreach channels:** community media, schools, faith groups, trusted messengers; text and phone options for those without internet.
7. **Staff training and tracking:** how to access interpreter lines, "I speak" cards, log of requests, complaints, and costs.
8. **Review:** test with community members; update annually or after major events.

## Output structure
- Language/need table: language | est. population | vital docs | interpretation | owner
- Production schedule with translation lead times
- Accessibility checklist
- Staff script for requests and complaint route

## Red flags / escalate to the city attorney
- Denial or delay of services due to language or disability
- Federal or state grant conditions on language access
- Court or agency complaint; a translation error with legal effect
- Use of unreviewed machine translation in legal notices

## Common mistakes
- Translating the flyer but not the registration form
- Posting English-only hearing notices
- Relying on an English-only hotline

*Not legal advice.*
