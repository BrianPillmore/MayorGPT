# Engagement Plan Designer

**Purpose:** Plan engagement before a decision is made, so input can still change the outcome. Match depth to the stakes, using a spectrum such as inform, consult, involve, collaborate, and decide (IAP2 framework) rather than defaulting to a single open house.

## Required inputs
- Decision to be made, who decides, and the decision date
- What is fixed (law, budget, safety) vs. what can change
- Affected groups, past controversy, and previous engagement
- Budget, staff, and timeline; legal notice duties (hearings, mailed notice, comment periods)

## Workflow
1. **Define the promise.** Write what the city will do with input ("We will consider all input and publish how it shaped the recommendation by ___"). Never overpromise influence.
2. **Stakeholder map:** directly affected, interested, those usually missed (renters, workers, youth, non-English speakers, people with disabilities), and decision makers. Consider geographic and demographic data to see who is absent.
3. **Choose methods per audience and level:** inform (mailers, web page, FAQ), consult (survey, comment period, open house), involve (workshops, advisory group), collaborate (task force, co-design). Pair online with in-person options and meet people where they are.
4. **Legal layer:** statutory notices and hearings are the floor, not the plan. Advisory groups may be subject to open-meetings law; check membership and quorum (see `open-meeting-risk-check`). Avoid advocacy for ballot measures with public funds (verify).
5. **Schedule backward from the decision date:** allow time for analysis of input (see `public-comment-summarizer`), response, and staff report drafting.
6. **Access:** language and disability access (see `language-access-plan`), childcare, time and place, compensation for participation where feasible.
7. **Messages and materials:** FAQ (see `faq-builder`), clear maps, plain summary, neutral survey wording (no leading questions); collect comparable demographic data.
8. **Evaluate:** participation counts vs. community makeup, quality of input, satisfaction, and what changed because of it. Publish a "you said, we did" summary.

## Output structure
- One-page plan: purpose, promise, decision timeline
- Stakeholder and method matrix: group | level | method | date | owner
- Notice and hearing calendar
- Budget and risks; evaluation metrics

## Red flags / escalate to the city attorney
- Predetermined outcome presented as consultation
- Advisory body forming that may be a covered public body
- Survey or incentive design that could be viewed as campaigning
- Data collection with privacy implications

## Common mistakes
- Engaging after the decision is effectively made
- Counting online clicks as representative
- No report-back to participants

*Not legal advice.*
