## Purpose
Prepare minutes that are an accurate, legally sufficient record of actions taken, not a transcript. Minutes are an official, permanent or long-retention record, usually public, and may be evidence of valid action under the open meetings law.

## Required inputs
- Agenda and packet, notice of meeting (posting evidence), and the roll of members.
- Clerk notes, recording with timestamps, and any chat or sign-in sheets.
- Text of motions, ordinances, resolutions, and amendments as adopted.
- Rules of procedure (adopted parliamentary authority, local rules) and the state open meetings law.
- Prior minutes format and the retention schedule.

## Workflow
1. Open with the heading: body, date, time called to order, location, presiding officer, recorder, and type of meeting (regular, special, emergency, work session, executive session).
2. Attendance: members present, absent, late arrivals and departures with times, staff, and attorney.
3. Record each action: for every motion write the mover, seconder (if required), the exact motion language, any amendment, discussion only in summary, and the vote with each member's vote on roll-call items. Distinguish passed, failed, tabled, and withdrawn.
4. Executive or closed sessions: record the time, the statutory basis cited for closing, the members present, the time of return, and any action taken in open session afterward. Do not summarize confidential content.
5. Public comment and hearings: record that comment was taken, number of speakers, and a neutral summary or names if the rules require them; for formal hearings, document required findings, notice, and testimony received into the record as exhibits.
6. Items adopted by reference: ordinance or resolution numbers, consent agenda contents, contracts approved with amounts and funding source, and conflict-of-interest recusals with reason.
7. Tone and content: neutral, past tense, no characterization of speakers' motives, no verbatim debate unless the body requires it. Corrections to prior minutes recorded when approved.
8. Quality check against the recording for votes, amounts, names, and numbers; flag items needing clerk verification as bracketed notes; route through the clerk and attorney as required, mark as draft until approved.

## Output structure
Minutes in standard order (call to order, roll, approval of agenda and prior minutes, consent, public comment, hearings, action items, reports, executive session, adjournment) followed by a clerk's verification list of uncertain items. See minutes-template.md.

## Red flags / escalate
- Action taken without proper notice, quorum, or in closed session.
- Votes unclear or inconsistent with the recording.
- Missing conflict-of-interest disclosures or recusals.
- Executive session basis not stated.
- Requests to alter approved minutes beyond clerical correction.
- Sensitive personnel or legal content that should stay out of minutes.

## Common mistakes
- Writing a transcript.
- Omitting the exact motion text.
- Failing to record who abstained or left the room.
- Including unverified claims by speakers as fact.
- Publishing before approval without a draft label.

## State-law variance
Content requirements, timing of release, executive session procedures, and recording obligations differ by state open meetings and records laws.
