## Purpose
Turn scattered studies, peer-city examples, and stakeholder input into a short options memo for a specific local decision. The memo should reflect the strength of evidence, not just the volume of sources, and should not advocate beyond what the evidence supports.

## Required inputs
- The decision question, decision-maker, authority (what the city can lawfully do), and timeline.
- Local context: data, existing policies, budget, and prior attempts.
- Source materials or search scope: peer-reviewed studies, government evaluations, think-tank reports, municipal league guidance, and peer city ordinances.
- Stakeholder positions and constraints.

## Workflow
1. Frame the question narrowly (what outcome, for whom, in what time) and confirm legal authority and preemption before researching options.
2. Collect sources and rate evidence quality: systematic review or experimental/quasi-experimental study (stronger), observational or before-and-after (moderate), case study, advocacy, or opinion (weaker). Note funder bias and publication date. Verify each citation exists before using it; do not fabricate sources or figures.
3. Extract what is known, uncertain, and contested. Distinguish effect size from statistical significance and note the transferability to the city's size, context, and capacity.
4. Develop three to five options, including "do nothing / monitor" and a lower-cost pilot. For each: mechanism, evidence, cost (one-time and recurring), staff and legal requirements, time to effect, risks and unintended effects, and equity effects.
5. Peer examples: describe two or three with context differences, results reported, and who evaluated them. Note failures.
6. Implementation questions: required ordinance or policy changes, procurement, data systems, enforcement, communication, and measurement plan with baseline and metrics.
7. Political and public-process considerations: stakeholders, likely concerns, hearing or referendum requirements.
8. Recommendation or decision criteria: make the trade-offs explicit; if recommending, state confidence level and what new evidence would change it.

## Output structure
| Option | Mechanism | Evidence strength | Cost (one-time/recurring) | Legal/authority | Risks | Time to effect |
Add: one-page summary, evidence table (source, type, finding, limitations), peer examples, implementation checklist, and open questions.

## Red flags / escalate
- Option conflicts with state preemption or constitutional limits.
- Heavy reliance on advocacy sources or single studies.
- Promised savings without an independent estimate.
- Policy affecting protected classes or civil rights.
- Missing citations or figures that cannot be verified.

## Common mistakes
- Presenting peer city press releases as evidence.
- Ignoring implementation capacity.
- Offering only one palatable option.
- Overstating certainty.

## State-law variance
Preemption, home-rule authority, and referendum requirements vary by state; confirm with the city attorney.
