## Purpose
Help the records officer respond completely and on time to a public records (open records, FOIA-equivalent) request. This skill organizes the work and drafts correspondence; the records officer and city attorney decide exemptions, redactions, and legal questions.

## Required inputs
- The request as received (date, time, channel, requester identity if given) and any prior communication.
- State public records statute: response deadlines, definitions of a record, exemptions, fee rules, and penalties.
- Likely custodians, systems (email, chat/text, case systems, cloud, AI tool logs, personal devices), and the retention schedule.
- Any related litigation hold, ongoing investigation, or third-party confidentiality (contracts, trade secrets).

## Workflow
1. Log the request and start the statutory clock immediately (acknowledgment deadline, response deadline, extension rights). Calendar them.
2. Clarify scope: if ambiguous, send a prompt clarification request that does not impose a purpose requirement; avoid delaying beyond what the statute allows. Reasonable narrowing may be offered but not forced.
3. Identify record locations and custodians, including email, messaging apps, text messages, social media, shared drives, vendor-held records, and AI tool logs where they exist. Issue a search instruction in writing with search terms and date range; require custodians to certify the search.
4. Preserve responsive records: suspend routine disposition for the responsive records until the request closes, plus any appeal period.
5. Review for exemptions: personnel, medical, attorney-client privilege, pending investigations, security plans, personal identifiers, trade secrets, deliberative process (where recognized). Apply the narrowest redaction needed, cite the specific statutory basis, and keep an exemption log. Do not use general discretion to withhold; segregate and release non-exempt portions.
6. Third-party notice: where law or contract requires notice to persons whose information is included, send it with the time to object.
7. Fees and installments: calculate only allowed fees (copying, staff time per statute), estimate for large requests, and provide records in installments with a schedule. Do not charge for redaction review where the law prohibits it.
8. Respond in writing: what is provided, what is withheld and why (citation), the number of pages, how to appeal, and the closing date. Retain the file (request, search records, log, response) under the records schedule.

## Output structure
| Step | Date due | Owner | Status |
Add: request log entry, search plan (custodian, system, terms, date), exemption log (record, basis, redaction), fee estimate, and draft response letter.

## Red flags / escalate
- Statutory deadline within a few days or already missed.
- Requests from media, litigants, or about the city attorney, elected officials, or employees: involve the city attorney.
- Privileged or investigative records.
- Records on personal accounts or devices of officials.
- Possible destruction after the request was received: stop and report.
- Mass or harassing requests: use statutory remedies only through counsel.

## Common mistakes
- Treating a request as a question to answer rather than a records request.
- Searching only email.
- Over-redacting or failing to log exemptions.
- Letting the clock run while waiting for clarification.
- Charging unauthorized fees.

## State-law variance
Deadlines, exemptions, fee rules, penalties, and appeal paths differ widely by state; always cite the state statute.
