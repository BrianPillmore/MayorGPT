## Purpose
Show, with citations to the adopted plan text, how a proposal supports, conflicts with, or is silent on the comprehensive plan. This supports findings of consistency required in many states for zoning decisions, capital programs, and plan amendments.

## Required inputs
- The adopted plan (current adopted version and amendment history), including future land use map, goals, policies, and implementation actions.
- Related adopted plans: transportation, parks, housing, hazard mitigation, utility master plans, subarea or corridor plans, and CIP.
- The proposal text or application and its site/area.
- Statutory standard for consistency in the state (must conform, be consistent with, or consider).

## Workflow
1. Establish the legal weight of the plan in this state and city: binding consistency requirement, advisory guide, or foundation for zoning. State it.
2. Map the proposal to the plan's future land use designation, density/intensity ranges, and any overlay or growth-tier policy.
3. Extract the relevant goals and policies by number and quote short phrases only. Do not paraphrase to create support that the text does not give.
4. Rate each: Supports, Partially supports, Neutral, Conflicts, or Silent. Record the plan citation and the evidence.
5. Identify internal plan conflicts (policies that point opposite directions) and say which controls under the plan's own interpretation rules.
6. Test implementation: does the plan require infrastructure, adequate public facilities, or phasing the proposal does not provide? Is the proposal needed to carry out a plan action item?
7. Check plan currency: is the plan outdated relative to growth, a new state requirement, or adopted functional plans? A proposal consistent with a stale plan may still be unwise.
8. Conclude: consistent, consistent with conditions, inconsistent (requires plan amendment or denial), or plan unclear. List the plan amendment or condition needed.

## Output structure
| Plan section/policy # | Text or summary (short) | Rating | Evidence from proposal | Condition or mitigation |

Add: consistency finding sentence, future-land-use match table, and a list of plan gaps.

## Red flags / escalate
- Proposal needs a plan amendment before zoning (process and notice differ).
- Plan citations that cannot be verified.
- Conflicting adopted plans.
- Applicant claims consistency based on a draft or superseded plan.
- Any consistency finding that will be a legal basis for denial or approval: city attorney review.

## Common mistakes
- Cherry-picking supportive policies and ignoring conflicting ones.
- Treating aspirational goals as binding standards.
- Skipping the future land use map.
- Using the applicant's summary as the plan's text.

## State-law variance
Whether a plan is binding or advisory, consistency standards, and amendment frequency limits vary by state.
