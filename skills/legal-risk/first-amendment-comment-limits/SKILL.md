# First Amendment Comment Limits

**Purpose:** Help the chair, clerk, and staff set and apply public-comment and online-moderation rules that respect speech rights. Courts analyze government forums by type; this is a screening aid and **the city attorney must confirm current case law in your circuit**.

## Required inputs
- The rule or incident (what was said, by whom, where, and what the chair did)
- Adopted public-comment rules and decorum policy; agenda context
- Platform and moderation policy if social media is involved

## Workflow
1. **Identify the forum.** Council public-comment periods are generally treated as limited public forums: the city may set reasonable limits on time, place, and manner and on subject matter tied to the body's business, but must stay viewpoint-neutral. Hearings on a specific agenda item may be confined to that item.
2. **Test the restriction:** content-neutral? narrowly tied to a legitimate purpose (orderly meeting)? Viewpoint discrimination (allowing praise but not criticism of officials) is the most dangerous category.
3. **Permissible tools (verify):** equal time limits, sign-up procedures, group time-pooling, limits on repetitive or off-topic speech, rules against actual disruption, threats, or true harassment, removal of speakers who violate neutral rules after warning.
4. **Risky tools:** bans on "personal attacks" or "disparagement" of officials or staff, prohibitions based on topic within the open comment period, advance approval of content, banning individuals from all meetings, and restricting criticism of the chair.
5. **Remedy ladder:** neutral reminder of rules; warning; mute or recess; removal limited to the disruption; documented, proportionate, equal application. Record the reason in minutes.
6. **Online:** official pages and comment threads can be forums; hiding or blocking critics based on viewpoint creates risk. Use written, neutral moderation rules (spam, threats, obscenity, off-topic by objective criteria) and keep a log.
7. **Accommodation:** ADA access, interpretation, remote comment parity.
8. **Training:** brief script for the chair (see `public-hearing-prep`).

## Output structure
- Plain-language answer: Likely fine / Risky / Escalate
- Forum classification and key facts
- Revised neutral rule or chair script
- Documentation checklist

## Red flags / escalate to the city attorney
- Removal for criticism of an official; ban of a named resident
- Police involvement or arrest; trespass notices
- Demand letter or threatened suit; media attention
- Hate speech, threats, or doxxing (separate legal analysis)

## Common mistakes
- Citing "decorum" to stop viewpoints
- Enforcing rules against one side only
- Treating the clerk's discretion as unreviewable
