# Franchise and Utility Review

**Purpose:** Frame the legal and process questions around franchises, rights-of-way use, rates, and city-owned utilities. State law, utility commissions, and federal rules often limit local authority, especially for cable, telecom, wireless facilities, and investor-owned electric and gas. **Many of these are preempted or heavily regulated; the attorney must confirm what the city can actually do.**

## Required inputs
- Franchise or action proposed, provider name, and current agreement (expiration, renewal terms)
- State statute and charter provisions on franchises, rights-of-way, and utility regulation
- Whether the city owns the utility or the provider is regulated by a state commission
- Financial terms: franchise fee, in-kind obligations, rate proposals

## Workflow
1. **Identify the authority**: municipal franchise power, rights-of-way ordinance, state utility commission jurisdiction, federal limits (for example on cable fees and wireless siting; verify). Determine whether the action is a franchise grant, renewal, transfer, rate change, or ordinance.
2. **Procedure:** many charters require an ordinance, multiple readings, public hearing, published notice, and sometimes voter approval for franchises of a certain length. Check the referendum window and effective date.
3. **Terms review:** term length and renewal, exclusivity (often barred), fee base and percentage, audit rights, indemnity and insurance, relocation and restoration duties, service area and build-out, customer service standards, transfer or change-of-control consent, default and revocation.
4. **Rates:** is the city the rate regulator, or only a party before the state commission? Check notice, hearing, and cost-of-service requirements for municipally owned utilities, and any pass-through of the general fund transfer.
5. **Rights-of-way:** permits, bonding, coordination with street projects, undergrounding, pole attachments, and small-cell or wireless-siting shot clocks (verify).
6. **Public engagement:** customer notice, comment, accessibility, and equity effects.
7. **Records and confidentiality:** vendor-claimed trade secrets versus disclosure laws.

## Output structure
- Authority and procedure map (with items marked "attorney to confirm")
- Terms table: provision | proposed | city preference | risk
- Timeline to expiration or deadlines (including shot clocks)
- Questions for the provider and for outside counsel

## Red flags / escalate to the city attorney
- Preemption or federal limits; rate matters in front of a state commission
- Exclusive rights, long terms, or perpetual easements
- Change of control or assignment without consent
- Deadlines that deem approval if the city does not act

## Common mistakes
- Letting a franchise expire with no holdover terms
- Agreeing to fees the state caps
- Missing deemed-granted deadlines
