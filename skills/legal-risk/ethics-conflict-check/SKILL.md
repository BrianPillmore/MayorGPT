# Ethics and Conflict Check

**Purpose:** Identify conflicts before a vote or award. Standards come from state ethics law, the city code of ethics, and the charter, and they differ in what counts as an "interest," the family-member circle, and the cure. **The ethics officer or city attorney decides; this skill only spots issues.**

## Required inputs
- Official involved, role, and the decision or action at stake
- Relationship facts: ownership, employment, family, debts, gifts, campaign contributions, prior advice given
- Applicable ethics code, disclosure filings, and any prior advisory opinion

## Workflow
1. **Financial interest test:** does the official, a relative, a business partner, or an employer stand to gain or lose differently than the general public? Check thresholds (ownership percent, income amount, distance from property) in your law.
2. **Contract interest:** many states bar officials from having a financial interest in city contracts they can influence, with narrow exceptions (verify). Procurement involvement also matters.
3. **Gifts and travel:** value limits, reporting, sources with matters before the city, gifts to family; exceptions for ceremonial gifts.
4. **Personal relationship and bias:** quasi-judicial roles require impartiality; disclose ex parte contacts; prior public statements may show prejudgment.
5. **Revolving door and outside work:** post-employment bans, moonlighting approvals, use of city resources.
6. **Campaign and political activity:** contributions from parties before the body (some jurisdictions require recusal after a threshold), use of public resources for campaigns.
7. **Select the remedy:** disclose on the record, recuse (leave the dais and do not participate), seek a written advisory opinion, remove from procurement team, divest, or request a waiver where the law allows. Confirm effect on quorum and the vote count if a member recuses.
8. **Document:** record the disclosure and recusal in the minutes with the nature of the interest as the law requires.

## Output structure
- Facts and assumptions
- Issue table: rule question | analysis | confidence | remedy
- Recommended action and script for the record
- Whether to obtain a formal advisory opinion

## Red flags / escalate to the city attorney
- Official's own financial stake; family employed by a vendor or applicant
- Gifts from interested parties; any quid pro quo suggestion
- Possible criminal exposure (bribery, self-dealing, misuse of office)
- An official wishes to "advise but not vote"

## Common mistakes
- Treating "appearance" issues as harmless when the code regulates them
- Recusing after speaking on the item
- Forgetting the vote-count effect on supermajority actions
