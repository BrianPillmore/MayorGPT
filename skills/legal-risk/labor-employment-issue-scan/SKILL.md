# Labor and Employment Issue Scan

**Purpose:** Spot employment-law risks early so HR and the city attorney can act before discipline, investigation, or a public statement makes things worse. Rules depend on employee status, state civil-service law, and union contracts. **This is not legal advice.**

## Required inputs
- Facts: who, what, when, witnesses, documents, prior similar events
- Employee status: at-will or protected, civil service, probationary, unionized (and which unit), elected, contractor
- Personnel policies, the collective bargaining agreement, and any civil-service rules
- Complaints already filed (internal, agency, union grievance)

## Workflow
1. **Classify the issue:** discipline or termination, harassment or discrimination, retaliation or whistleblower, leave and accommodation, wage-hour, safety, bargaining obligations, or public-statement risk.
2. **Due process:** public employees with a property interest in continued employment typically need notice of charges and a chance to respond before significant discipline (verify civil-service and contract steps). Check timelines for investigations and discipline.
3. **Protected activity and retaliation:** complaints, whistleblowing, union activity, leave use, workers' compensation claims, jury duty. Adverse action soon after protected activity is a classic claim.
4. **Discrimination/harassment:** protected classes under federal and state law; employer duty to investigate promptly and impartially; interim measures; avoid investigators who are conflicted.
5. **Leave and accommodation:** federal and state family and medical leave, disability accommodation (interactive process), pregnancy and military leave, workers' compensation overlap.
6. **Wage and hour:** exempt/non-exempt classification, overtime rules for public safety and fire work periods, comp time, off-the-clock work, on-call.
7. **Bargaining:** unilateral changes to mandatory subjects may require meeting and conferring with the union first (verify under state law).
8. **First Amendment and public statements:** employee speech on matters of public concern has limits; officials should not publicly comment on pending personnel matters.
9. **Records and holds:** personnel files are often partly confidential; start preserving evidence (see `litigation-hold-checklist`).
10. **Closed session:** personnel discussion may be closed under state law only for specific individuals and subjects (see `executive-session-checklist`).

## Output structure
- Issue classification and employee status
- Risk table: issue | rule area | severity | needed action
- Immediate steps and deadlines (filing, grievance, investigation)
- Do/don't list for managers

## Red flags / escalate to the city attorney
- Allegations against the manager, mayor, or a council member
- Safety, criminal conduct, or immigration issues
- Termination of someone who recently complained or took leave
- Agency charge, grievance, demand letter, or press inquiry

## Common mistakes
- Discipline without a documented investigation
- Discussing a pending matter in open session or by group text
- Ignoring a union's right to representation

*Not legal advice.*
