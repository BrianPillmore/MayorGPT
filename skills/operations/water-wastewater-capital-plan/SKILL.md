## Purpose
Create a defensible multi-year utility capital plan that ranks projects by risk and regulatory need, matches funding to projects, and shows rate impacts. This skill supports engineers and finance; it does not replace licensed engineering review or regulatory counsel.

## Required inputs
- Asset inventory and condition (pipe age/material/breaks, pump and lift station condition, treatment plant process condition, storage, meters, hydrants, manholes, CCTV inspection results).
- Regulatory profile: discharge or drinking water permits, compliance schedules, consent orders, lead service line inventory and replacement obligations, PFAS or other emerging rules, sanitary sewer overflow history, and combined sewer controls.
- Master plans, hydraulic models, capacity and growth projections, and I&I (inflow and infiltration) studies.
- Existing debt, reserves, rate structure, and prior rate study (see enterprise-fund-rate-study).
- Funding options: revolving fund loans (clean water and drinking water programs), grants, revenue bonds, developer contributions, and cash.

## Workflow
1. Separate drivers: regulatory/mandated, renewal of failing assets, capacity for growth, resilience/redundancy, and efficiency. Growth projects should be funded by growth where lawful (impact fees, capacity fees).
2. Risk-rank assets using likelihood of failure (condition, age, material, break history) times consequence (customers affected, critical customers, environmental damage, service interruption, repair difficulty). Show the matrix and criticality assumptions.
3. Lifecycle strategy: choose rehabilitate vs. replace, trenchless vs. open cut, and sequencing with street and other utility projects ("dig once"). Estimate annual replacement need by pipe class and compare to current replacement rate (percent of network per year).
4. Capacity: check peak flows, wet-weather performance, storage, and treatment loading with growth projections; identify bottlenecks before approving development.
5. Regulatory timeline: translate each mandate into a project, deadline, and cost; flag where a schedule slip risks penalties or enforcement.
6. Cost and delivery: cost class and estimate date, contingency, delivery method (design-bid-build, design-build, CMAR) permitted by state law, permitting, and workforce capacity. Do not stack more projects than engineering and construction can deliver.
7. Funding plan: match each project to funding by eligibility and timing, anticipate loan application cycles and environmental review, and calculate debt service. Test rate impact and coverage with the enterprise rate study; present affordability.
8. Resilience and operations: power backup, flood protection, cybersecurity of control systems, emergency interconnects, and O&M impact of new assets.

## Output structure
| Project | Driver | Risk score | Regulatory deadline | Cost (class/date) | Funding | Year | Rate impact |
Add: replacement-rate table, five and ten year funding summary, risks, and decision points. 

## Red flags / escalate
- Enforcement actions, missed compliance dates, or overflows.
- Lead service lines or contamination findings.
- Critical single points of failure.
- Funding gap leaves mandated work unfunded.
- Capital plan relying on unconfirmed grants or loans.
- Cyber vulnerabilities in SCADA.

## Common mistakes
- Prioritizing growth over renewal.
- Ignoring I&I.
- Underestimating inflation in out-year costs.
- Setting rates that cannot support debt covenants.
- Poor coordination with street projects.

## State-law variance
Loan program rules, delivery methods, rate-setting procedures, and permit requirements vary by state.
