## Purpose
Create a neutral, fact-based case summary that shows where a code enforcement matter stands in the legal process and what the next lawful step is. It is a working draft for the officer, supervisor, and city attorney, not the evidentiary record.

## Required inputs
- Case file: complaint source and date, property address and parcel, owner and occupant (including registered agent), inspection reports with date/time, photos and field notes, and officer identity.
- Code sections alleged, notices issued (type, method of service, dates, proof of service), compliance deadlines, and responses from the owner.
- Prior cases at the property, liens, taxes owed, vacant/abandoned registry status, and any pending permit.
- Safety indicators: structural hazards, unsafe occupancy, fire or health risk, vulnerable occupants.
- Applicable procedure: administrative hearing, citation, abatement, receivership, or court action.

## Workflow
1. Establish facts: observed violation, location, dates, and evidence source. Separate officer observations from complainant statements and from inferences.
2. Verify jurisdictional basis: right code section, owner of record, proper notice (statutory method and time), and whether a warrant is required for entry beyond what is visible from public areas.
3. Status in the process: complaint, first notice, reinspection, notice of violation, hearing, order, citation or fine, abatement, lien, or referral. State the next deadline and who acts.
4. Risk triage: immediate danger (structural collapse, fire, gas, unsafe occupancy) is handled through emergency abatement or unsafe-building procedures and may bypass normal steps; routine conditions follow the standard cycle. Consider the vulnerability of occupants (displacement, tenant rights, relocation obligations).
5. Fairness and consistency: compare to similar cases, check for selective enforcement, accessibility needs, language access, hardship or compliance-assistance programs, and any retaliation concerns.
6. Cost and remedies: abatement costs, fine schedule, lien priority, collection method, and limits on fines under state law. Confirm that cost recovery is lawful before the city performs work.
7. Recommended routing: continue, issue notice, schedule hearing, send to city attorney, emergency abatement, close as compliant, or refer to services (rehab or repair assistance).
8. Evidence handling: tie each claim to a photo, report, or document with a date; keep the officer's original notes.

## Output structure
| Date | Event | Source/evidence | Code section | Status/next deadline |
Add: summary paragraph, violation list, notice-service check, risk level, recommended action, and open questions for the officer.

## Red flags / escalate
- Life-safety hazard or vulnerable occupants: escalate same day to building official, fire, and the city attorney.
- Defective notice or service; deadlines near expiration.
- AI used to pick properties or people for inspection: the selection criteria and data must be documented and reviewed by the city attorney before operational use; "the AI flagged it" is not an answer on challenge.
- AI-generated summary offered as evidence: it is a draft only. The inspecting officer must verify against personal observations before it enters the file or any hearing.
- Possible discrimination or retaliation claim; owner hardship or disability; criminal activity (route to police).
- Demolition, lien foreclosure, or receivership: attorney-led.

## Common mistakes
- Treating the complaint as proof.
- Skipping reinspection before escalation.
- Mixing opinion and observation.
- Applying a code section not adopted or in force at the time.
- Ignoring owner contact attempts in the record.

## State-law variance
Notice, warrant, lien, fine caps, and unsafe-building procedures vary by state and by city charter.
