## Purpose
Produce timely, accurate, plain-language messages for planned and unplanned utility disruptions, including boil-water advisories, sewer overflows, and main breaks. Public health notifications for drinking water and sewage releases have regulatory requirements and must not be improvised.

## Required inputs
- Event type, affected area (map/addresses/count of customers), start time, cause if known, and estimated restoration time with confidence level.
- Utility operations lead's technical summary and any water quality sampling data.
- Regulatory triggers: drinking water rules, discharge permit conditions, state notification timelines, and health department requirements.
- Vulnerable customers: hospitals, dialysis centers, schools, care facilities, and registered medical-need customers.
- Communication channels: alert system, website, social media, door hangers, media, and language needs.

## Workflow
1. Determine the message type: planned shutoff (advance notice, 48 hours or per policy), emergency outage, precautionary boil water advisory, boil water order, do-not-use or do-not-drink order, sewer overflow/spill advisory, or all-clear.
2. Check mandatory notifications: for drinking water events, public notification rules specify tiers and timelines (tier 1 urgent notice within a short period) and required content; for sewer overflows, permit notification to the state regulator and possibly public/health warnings. Confirm requirements with the utility's regulatory contact and state primacy agency; do not rely on this skill for the legal text.
3. Draft the core: what happened, who is affected (address-level if possible), what to do (use bottled water, boil one minute per local guidance, avoid flushing, conserve water), what not to do, how long, and where to get help (water distribution points, hotline).
4. Include health protections: infants, medical devices, dialysis, food service, and pets. Use official health agency language where available.
5. Restoration language: give estimated time with confidence ("crews expect to restore service by 6 p.m.; we will update by 3 p.m."). Do not guess; say when the next update will come.
6. Tailor versions: alert/text (160 characters), social post, website banner, press release, door hanger, and internal talking points. Provide Spanish or other local languages and accessibility formats.
7. Coordinate with the PIO/EOC, public safety, health department, schools, and neighboring utilities. Notify elected officials before the public when possible.
8. Plan updates and closure: re-test results, all-clear criteria, and a post-event summary with lessons learned.

## Output structure
| Channel | Message | Audience | Send time | Approver |
Add: key facts table (event, area, cause, ETA, instructions, contact), FAQ, and a resolution message. See outage-message-template.md.

## Red flags / escalate
- Contamination or positive bacteriological samples: public health authority and state regulator immediately.
- Sewage in streets or waterways.
- Vulnerable facilities affected.
- Conflicting restoration times.
- Cyber or intentional contamination suspected: law enforcement and state/federal partners.
- Misinformation spreading; legal exposure for statements on cause.

## Common mistakes
- Speculating on cause.
- Using technical terms ("pressure transient") without explanation.
- Announcing restoration too early.
- Leaving off lifting criteria for advisories.
- Forgetting non-English speakers.

## State-law variance
Public notification deadlines and content for water and sewer events are set by state primacy agencies and permits.
