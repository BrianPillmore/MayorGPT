## Purpose
Produce an accurate, plain-language progress update for council and residents that reports schedule, budget, scope changes, and what residents will experience, without overpromising dates.

## Required inputs
- Project name, location, contractor, contract amount, notice-to-proceed and substantial completion dates, and funding sources.
- Engineer or inspector progress report: percent complete (physical and financial), approved change orders and pending claims, weather days, and critical-path issues.
- Traffic control plan, detours, utility shutoffs, driveway access, and work hours.
- Budget status: original contract, change orders, contingency, and total project budget including design and inspection.
- Public information needs: stakeholders, complaints, and communications channels.

## Workflow
1. Verify the numbers with the project manager and engineer, not just the contractor's pay request. State the report date.
2. Schedule: planned vs. forecast substantial and final completion, with variance explained (weather, utility conflicts, supply, design changes). Differentiate contractual completion from public re-opening dates. Include liquidated damages status only as relevant and as confirmed by the attorney.
3. Budget: contract amount, approved change orders (count and total, percent of contract), contingency remaining, pending change orders, and forecast at completion. Note funding source status and grant reimbursement requirements.
4. Scope and quality: changes, unforeseen conditions, inspection or test results, and warranty/retainage.
5. Resident impact: closures, detours, parking, noise, water or sewer interruptions, trash/mail, school and emergency access, accessibility. Give dates with confidence levels and who to call.
6. Safety and incidents: injuries, near misses, and public complaints with response status.
7. Upcoming milestones and decisions: council actions (change orders over authority, final acceptance), and the next update date.
8. Draft two forms: a council memo with detail and a short public notice in plain English and translated if needed; keep tone calm and factual.

## Output structure
| Item | Planned | Current forecast | Variance | Reason |
Rows: substantial completion, final completion, contract amount, change orders, contingency, total project cost.
Add: next 30-day look-ahead, resident impact table (date, location, impact, workaround), and issues requiring decisions. See project-update-template.md.

## Red flags / escalate
- Cost forecast over budget or change orders approaching the authority limit.
- Claims, delay disputes, or contractor default: city attorney.
- Utility strikes, safety incidents, or environmental violations.
- Grant deadlines or federal compliance (Davis-Bacon payroll, Buy America) at risk.
- Emergency access blocked.

## Common mistakes
- Quoting the contractor's optimistic date.
- Omitting soft costs.
- Using technical jargon in resident notices.
- Announcing dates before weather and utility risk are assessed.

## State-law variance
Change order authority, retainage, prompt payment, and notice rules vary by state and local policy.
