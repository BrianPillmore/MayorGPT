## Purpose
Prioritize a backlog of building and facility issues (roofs, HVAC, electrical, fire/life safety, ADA, plumbing, envelope, security) so limited maintenance and capital dollars address life safety and mission-critical systems first, and so deferred work is documented with its risk.

## Required inputs
- Issue list with building, system, description, age, and work order history.
- Facility condition assessments or inspection reports; fire marshal and insurance carrier findings; ADA transition plan.
- Building use and criticality (police/fire/EOC, 24/7 operations, public service counters, storage).
- Cost estimates and funding source (operating maintenance, building fund, CIP, grants).
- Warranty and service contracts.

## Workflow
1. Normalize each issue: system, location, failure mode, and consequence of failure (injury, loss of service, damage, code violation).
2. Score with a transparent rubric (adjust to policy): life safety/code 35, mission criticality 20, likelihood of failure/condition 20, cost escalation if deferred 10, regulatory or insurance exposure 10, cost-effectiveness 5. Show raw scores.
3. Classify the work type: emergency/reactive, corrective, preventive, deferred maintenance, or capital renewal. Preventive and life-safety items should not compete with discretionary upgrades.
4. Apply sequencing: fix the envelope (roof, drainage) before interior systems; consider co-locating work to reduce mobilization and downtime; confirm whether replacement will trigger code upgrades such as ADA, energy, or fire code.
5. Temporary workarounds: fencing, signage, fire watch, portable equipment, relocation, reduced occupancy. State the duration, cost, and who accepts the residual risk in writing.
6. Funding fit: operating vs. capital eligibility, restricted funds, enterprise facilities, grants, and energy performance contracting. Estimate lifecycle cost and lifespan.
7. Procurement path: emergency purchase authority and documentation, cooperative purchasing, competitive bids by threshold, prevailing wage, and abatement requirements (asbestos or lead in older buildings).
8. Present tiers: Do now, Next 6 to 12 months, Capital plan, Monitor, with the cost of delay.

## Output structure
| Rank | Facility | Issue | Safety/compliance | Mission impact | Score | Cost | Workaround | Funding | Target date |
Add: total by tier, five-year backlog and funding gap, deferred-risk log signed by department head, and quick wins.

## Red flags / escalate
- Fire alarm, sprinkler, egress, electrical hazards, or structural concerns: immediate action and notice to the fire marshal and building official.
- Mold, asbestos, lead, or water intrusion: environmental and HR/risk notification.
- Public safety or EOC facility single point of failure.
- Insurance or regulatory notice deadlines.
- Emergency work done without proper procurement documentation.

## Common mistakes
- Ranking by complaint volume rather than consequence.
- Ignoring warranty claims.
- Replacing parts of failing systems repeatedly.
- Forgetting code triggers.
- Not recording the accepted risk.

## State-law variance
Emergency procurement rules, prevailing wage, and abatement regulations vary by state.
