## Purpose
Give the mayor, manager, and council one accurate page in the first hours of an incident and an updated version at set intervals. The brief separates confirmed facts from reports and uses NIMS/ICS terms so it aligns with the Incident Commander and the EOC. It does not direct tactical operations.

## Required inputs
- Source of information (Incident Commander, EOC situation report, dispatch, field staff) and time of last update.
- Incident type and location, status (contained/uncontained), and ICS structure (Incident Commander or Unified Command, EOC activation level).
- Life safety status, injuries, evacuations and shelters, critical infrastructure and utility status, road closures.
- Resources committed and requested (mutual aid, county/state EMA), declarations in effect or being considered.
- Public information status: alerts sent, press statements, rumors.

## Workflow
1. Stamp the brief: date, time, operational period, version, source, and next update time. Write "unconfirmed" beside anything not verified by the IC or an official source.
2. Life safety first: casualties, missing, evacuated, sheltered, vulnerable populations, and care facilities. Use counts with source and time.
3. Situation: what happened, current size/scope, and trend (worsening, stable, improving) with the next expected change. No speculation about cause.
4. Impacts: lifelines (power, water, wastewater, communications, transportation, healthcare), critical facilities, businesses, schools, and city operations/continuity.
5. Actions underway: by ICS function (command, operations, planning, logistics, finance/admin) and by agency. Include the incident objectives for the current period.
6. Resource asks and decision points: what the city needs from leaders (emergency declaration, spending authority, mutual aid agreement, EOC staffing, shelter funding), with who decides and by when. Note legal authority for emergency purchases.
7. Messaging: facts safe to release, approved sources, what not to say (cause, blame, unconfirmed numbers), and the spokesperson. Coordinate through the Joint Information Center/PIO. Consider multilingual and accessible channels.
8. Cost and documentation: begin tracking staff hours, equipment, and purchases by incident code now to protect reimbursement (FEMA PA requires documentation; thresholds and rules change, verify). Preserve logs and ICS forms.
9. Next steps and the next update schedule.

## Output structure
Header block, then:
| Section | Content |
Rows: Life safety | Situation and trend | Impacts | Actions underway | Resource asks and decisions | Messaging | Costs/documentation | Next update
Add: confirmed vs. unconfirmed list. See incident-brief-template.md.

## Red flags / escalate
- Fatalities, mass-casualty events, hazardous materials, critical infrastructure failure, or cyber incident: notify the manager, city attorney, and appropriate state/federal partners immediately.
- Declaration or evacuation orders needing legal authority.
- Conflicting information between sources.
- Sensitive data (victim names, security vulnerabilities) in the brief: restrict distribution and check public records exemptions.
- Elected officials directing operations outside ICS.

## Common mistakes
- Mixing confirmed and rumored facts.
- Using local jargon instead of plain English or common terminology.
- Failing to record the time of each number.
- Forgetting continuity of normal city services.
- Releasing cause or fault early.

## State-law variance
Emergency declaration powers, open-meeting exceptions during emergencies, and public records exemptions vary by state.
