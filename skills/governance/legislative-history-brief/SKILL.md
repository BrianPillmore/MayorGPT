# Legislative History Brief

**Purpose:** Reconstruct what the city has actually done on a topic, in order, with sources, so officials do not rely on memory or folklore. Useful for amendments, disputes over intent, enforcement questions, and litigation support.

## Required inputs
- Topic, ordinance/resolution number, or code section
- Access to codified code history notes, agendas, minutes, staff reports, audio/video, and published notices
- Time span and any known amendments

## Workflow
1. **Identify the instrument and its history trail.** Code history notes usually list ordinance numbers and dates of each amendment; start there and confirm against adopted originals, not drafts.
2. **Build the timeline.** For each action record: date, body, instrument type and number, vote and who voted how, motion text, amendments adopted or rejected, effective date, and source document.
3. **Capture the stated purpose** as recorded: findings, recitals, staff-report rationale, key testimony. Quote sparingly and exactly; mark paraphrase as paraphrase.
4. **Track related actions:** implementing regulations, fee schedules, contracts, grants, court decisions or settlements, state law changes that triggered revisions.
5. **Note what is absent:** missing minutes, unavailable recordings, pages without attachments. Do not infer intent from silence.
6. **Check retention status:** are source records within retention? If litigation is possible, flag a hold (see `litigation-hold-checklist`).
7. **Distinguish** adopted law from proposals, tabled items, and failed motions.

## Output structure
1. Two-sentence bottom line
2. Timeline table: date | action | body | vote | source
3. Evolution of key provisions (before / after text for major amendments)
4. Stated purposes vs. current practice
5. Gaps, conflicts, and open questions

## Red flags / escalate to the city attorney
- Adopted text differs from the codified text
- Discrepancies between minutes and recordings
- The history will be used in an adversarial proceeding or a records dispute
- Records suggest an ordinance was never properly published or effective

## Common mistakes
- Relying on a staff summary instead of the adopted instrument
- Presenting one member's remark as the council's intent
- Mixing up resolution and ordinance numbering series

*Cite only documents actually reviewed; never fill gaps from assumption.*
