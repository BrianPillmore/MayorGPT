# Initiative, Referendum, and Recall Guide

**Purpose:** Produce a neutral, step-by-step process guide for residents and staff about a citizen-power procedure. Availability, signature thresholds, and subject-matter limits differ sharply by state and by charter; many cities have none of the three.

## Required inputs
- Which process (initiative, referendum, recall) and the specific measure or officer
- Charter and state-law text on petitions (ask for the sections)
- Clerk's current forms and verification rules
- Where the process currently stands (pre-circulation, circulating, filed, verification, certified)

## Workflow
1. **Confirm the process exists** and who can use it (registered voters, residents, property owners).
2. **Distinguish the three:** an initiative proposes new law; a referendum asks voters to approve or reject an adopted ordinance (note the short window after adoption, which can suspend the effective date); a recall seeks removal of an officer (check grounds, timing limits after taking office, and whether a vote is required to replace).
3. **List the steps in order:** notice of intent / filing of the proposed text, title and summary, review for form and legal sufficiency, circulation period, signature requirements (usually a percentage of voters or of a prior turnout, and sometimes distribution rules), submission, clerk verification (sampling vs. full count), cure periods, certification, council duty (adopt, submit to voters, or refer), ballot placement.
4. **Role of the city:** the clerk acts ministerially and neutrally. Staff must not advise how to win or lose, and city resources must not be used to advocate. Provide the same forms and information to all sides.
5. **Subject limits:** initiatives and referendums often cannot cover administrative acts, budgets, appropriations, or matters preempted by the state; this is a legal determination to route to the attorney, not the clerk.
6. **Calendar:** reverse-plan to the election date (see `election-calendar-planner`).

## Output structure
- One-page neutral overview (plain language, no advocacy)
- Step table: step | who | deadline | source (verify)
- FAQ for the clerk counter
- Open legal questions list

## Red flags / escalate to the city attorney
- Dispute over whether the measure is a proper subject
- Challenges to signatures, circulators, or petition format
- Any pressure to delay or accelerate certification
- Recall grounds or timing disputes involving the mayor or a member

## Common mistakes
- Missing the referendum window (it may run only days from adoption)
- Giving strategic advice to proponents or opponents
- Assuming one state's signature math applies elsewhere

*Neutral process information only; verify against charter and state law. Not legal advice.*
