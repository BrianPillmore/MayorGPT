# Public Hearing Prep

**Purpose:** Prepare the chair, staff, and clerk to run a hearing that satisfies notice, fairness, and record requirements. The type of hearing changes the rules: **legislative** (ordinances, budgets, fees) is broad and open; **quasi-judicial** (rezoning of a parcel, permits, variances, licenses, some personnel matters) requires due process, a record, and impartiality.

## Required inputs
- Hearing type and legal basis; applicant or parties
- Published and mailed notice proofs; dates and text
- Staff report and exhibits; local hearing rules and time limits

## Workflow
1. **Notice checkpoint:** published notice dates, mailed notice radius, posted-on-site notice, language, and content (time, place, subject, how to comment). Bad notice can void the action; verify requirements and obtain proof of publication and mailing.
2. **Classify the hearing** and select the script (see below).
3. **Quasi-judicial steps:** chair announces procedure; swear in witnesses if required; members disclose ex parte contacts and site visits; ask for objections to jurisdiction or bias; staff report; applicant presentation; public testimony; rebuttal; close the record; deliberation; findings; vote. Decision must rest on the record and adopted criteria.
4. **Legislative steps:** staff presentation, public testimony, close hearing, deliberate, act or continue.
5. **Public comment rules:** time limits applied evenly, sign-up cards, speaker order, group donation of time if allowed. Regulate time, place, and manner, not viewpoint; keep it content-neutral (see `first-amendment-comment-limits`). Provide accommodations (interpretation, ADA).
6. **Record capture:** minutes, recording, written submissions logged with time received, exhibits labeled. Late materials: decide by rule whether to accept or continue.
7. **Closing options:** approve, approve with conditions (with findings), deny with findings, continue to a date certain (announce date and time to avoid renotice), leave record open for written comment.

## Output structure
- Notice compliance checklist
- Chair's script (opening, ground rules, closing)
- Order-of-proceedings table with time allocations
- Comment capture sheet fields: name, address, position, topic, exhibit
- Motion options (see `motion-language-crafter`)

## Red flags / escalate to the city attorney
- Defective or late notice; pending litigation on the matter
- Member with financial interest, bias, or undisclosed ex parte contact
- Request to continue after the hearing is closed
- Applicant attorney raising vested-rights, takings, or procedural objections

## Common mistakes
- Letting the record close without announcing it
- Allowing new evidence after closing
- Failing to tie decisions to adopted criteria

*Verify notice and hearing rules in state law and local code. Not legal advice.*
