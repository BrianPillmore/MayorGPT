# Resolution Drafter

**Purpose:** Draft a clean resolution that matches what the body is legally permitted to do by resolution rather than ordinance. Use `resolution-template.md`.

## Required inputs
- The decision to be made, sponsor, and effective date
- The authority (charter, code section, statute, contract, or grant requirement) that allows or requires a resolution
- Attachments and exhibits; budget source if money is involved

## Workflow
1. **Test vehicle.** A resolution generally expresses policy or administrative action (approve a contract, appoint, adopt a fee schedule where permitted, make findings, accept a grant, call an election, commemorate). Binding regulation, penalties, and zoning text changes normally need an ordinance. If in doubt, flag it (see `charter-code-crosscheck`).
2. **Title:** short and specific; includes the action and key amount or subject.
3. **Recitals ("WHEREAS")**: facts only, in order: authority, background, public process held, staff recommendation, funding source. Each recital should be verifiable. Avoid argument.
4. **Resolving clauses ("NOW, THEREFORE BE IT RESOLVED")**: numbered, each one action: approve, authorize signature by title (not by name), set a not-to-exceed amount, appropriate funds or direct a budget amendment, direct staff, and set an effective date (usually on adoption).
5. **Findings:** for land use, environmental-review determinations, or public-purpose findings, state findings tied to criteria.
6. **Attachments:** list exhibits and require them to be final; "substantially in the form" language lets the manager finalize minor changes with attorney approval.
7. **Signature blocks:** presiding officer, attest by clerk, approved as to form by attorney. Include adoption date, motion, second, and roll-call vote.
8. **Records:** number from the clerk's series, filed in the permanent records.

## Output structure
- Resolution text
- Cover issues note: authority, vote needed, funding, open items
- Companion motion

## Example
> WHEREAS, the City Council finds that ... ; NOW, THEREFORE, BE IT RESOLVED that: 1. The agreement with ___ is approved in an amount not to exceed $___. 2. The City Manager is authorized to execute it in a form approved by the City Attorney.

## Red flags / escalate to the city attorney
- Resolution attempts to regulate private conduct or override code
- Open-ended spending authority or multi-year commitments
- Appointments without the required procedure
- Findings that are conclusory

## Common mistakes
- Naming an individual signer instead of an office
- Missing the funding source
- Recitals that assert facts not in the record

*Draft for attorney review; not legal advice.*
