# Open Meeting Risk Check

**Purpose:** Screen a scenario against open-meetings (sunshine) law before it happens. Rules vary by state; the Brown Act (California), Texas Open Meetings Act, Florida Sunshine Law, and others differ on definitions of "meeting," quorum counting, and remedies. **Verify everything against your state statute and the city attorney's guidance.**

## Required inputs
- Scenario (who, how many members, medium, topic, purpose, planned date)
- Body size and quorum; whether committees, advisory boards, and ad hoc groups are covered under your law
- Whether the topic is within the body's jurisdiction

## Workflow
1. **Is a covered body involved?** Council, boards, commissions, standing committees, and often ad hoc committees with delegated authority. Check state definitions.
2. **Is there a quorum, or a "collective" gathering?** Count members present or communicating. Some states reach below a majority (for example, committees of two members, or any gathering where deliberation occurs).
3. **Is business within jurisdiction being discussed, deliberated, or decided?** Social events are typically fine if no business is discussed; verify.
4. **Serial or walking-quorum risk:** A-to-B, B-to-C emails, texts, or staff-brokered "hub and spoke" conversations that together reach a majority can be a meeting. Reply-all on email threads and group chats are frequent triggers. Staff conveying positions between members is a classic pattern.
5. **Notice, agenda, and access:** posting period, content of the agenda, location accessibility, remote participation rules, recording rules, and public-comment opportunity. Actions on items not described on the agenda are risky.
6. **Exceptions:** closed sessions require a specific statutory ground and procedure (see `executive-session-checklist`); conferences and trainings, and "emergency" or "urgent" provisions have narrow criteria.
7. **Records:** texts and emails on personal devices may be public records regardless of the open-meetings result.
8. **Cure options:** cancel or restructure, reduce attendance below quorum, publish notice and agenda, move to a noticed session, or ask the attorney about cure and cease-and-desist procedures where your law has them.

## Output structure
- Risk rating: Low / Medium / High with the single main reason
- Facts used and assumptions
- Issue table: rule question | analysis | confidence (verify)
- Recommended safe alternative (reworded plan)
- Suggested guidance to members in two sentences

## Example
> Three of five members are in a group text "just to align" on a vote. Risk: High. A quorum is communicating about business within the body's jurisdiction. Move to staff memo plus public discussion.

## Red flags / escalate to the city attorney
- Prior violation allegations or demand letters
- Action taken at a possibly improper meeting (consider re-noticing and ratification; remedies vary)
- Staff acting as intermediary for member positions
- Any request to "decide ahead of time" a vote

## Common mistakes
- Assuming no-vote means no meeting
- Treating social media comments among members as outside the law
- Forgetting that advisory bodies are often covered
