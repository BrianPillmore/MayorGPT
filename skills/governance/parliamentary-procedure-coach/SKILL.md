# Parliamentary Procedure Coach

**Purpose:** Give the chair, member, or clerk the right move at the right moment. Most councils adopt Robert's Rules of Order (or a simplified variant) as a default, but **their own adopted rules and state law control**. Ask for the rules before advising.

## Required inputs
- Situation: what has been moved, what is pending, who has spoken
- The council's adopted rules of procedure and the edition of any manual adopted
- Quorum, vote threshold, and whether small-body relaxations apply (many councils allow the chair to move and debate; verify)

## Workflow
1. **Identify what is pending** (main motion, amendment, substitute, subsidiary or privileged motion).
2. **Apply the order of precedence.** Highest to lowest, roughly: adjourn, recess, point of privilege, then (subsidiary) table or lay on the table, previous question/call the question, limit debate, postpone to a time, refer to committee, amend, postpone indefinitely, then the main motion. A higher-ranking motion may interrupt a lower one.
3. **Check each motion's properties:** needs a second? debatable? amendable? vote needed (a motion to limit or end debate usually needs two-thirds; verify)?
4. **Quorum:** confirm at the start and when members leave. If quorum is lost, only limited actions (recess, adjourn, fix time) are valid.
5. **Conflicts and abstentions:** recusal is a legal duty tied to conflict-of-interest law (see `ethics-conflict-check`); a plain abstention is a choice and, under many rules, counts with the majority or is not counted. Verify and announce how it will be treated.
6. **Chair duties:** state the motion before debate; recognize speakers fairly; keep debate on the question; rule on points of order; call for a clear vote; announce the result.
7. **Reconsider and rescind:** reconsideration usually must be moved by a member on the prevailing side at the same or next meeting; rescinding or amending adopted actions may require notice or a larger vote; some actions already carried out cannot be undone.
8. **Fix errors:** if a vote was taken improperly, correct at once, on the record.

## Output structure
- Plain-language ruling and the exact words for the chair to say
- Why (rule source, to be confirmed)
- If wrong, the repair step

## Example
> Pending: motion to approve. A member says "I move to table." Chair: "A motion to table is not debatable and needs a second. Is there a second? ... All in favor of laying the item on the table." (Note: confirm your rules, since some bodies treat it as postponement.)

## Red flags / escalate to the city attorney
- Dispute over whether a vote carried or a quorum existed
- Use of procedure to evade the public-comment or notice requirement
- Chair ruling challenged and appealed from the chair

## Common mistakes
- Using "table" to mean "postpone"
- Voting on an amendment without stating the amendment
- Calling the question without a vote when a vote is required

*Local rules prevail; not legal advice.*
