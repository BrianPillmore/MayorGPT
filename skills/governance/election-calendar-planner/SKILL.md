# Election Calendar Planner

**Purpose:** Build a backward-planned municipal election calendar so the clerk and council meet every statutory deadline. Election dates and deadlines are set by state election law, the charter, and the county or state election authority; the city often only administers its own piece.

## Required inputs
- Offices and measures on the ballot; election date and method (in-person, vote by mail, runoff, ranked choice)
- Who administers (city clerk, county election office, contracted vendor)
- State election code deadlines and the county's published calendar (obtain from the election official; do not rely on memory)
- Charter provisions on terms, vacancies, and swearing-in

## Workflow
1. **Anchor on Election Day** and work backward. Typical milestones to verify: call-of-election resolution, ballot-measure language adoption, candidate filing period opens/closes, withdrawal and ballot-order deadlines, voter-registration cutoff, candidate-statement and ballot-argument deadlines, publication of notices, ballot certification, mail-ballot drop, logic and accuracy testing, canvass and certification, recount window, swearing-in.
2. **Separate city tasks from county/state tasks** and list the handoff dates and who signs.
3. **Council actions needed:** resolutions calling the election or measure, consolidation with another election, funding the cost, setting polling places. Place them on agendas early enough for required readings and posting.
4. **Candidate-facing items:** filing packets, campaign-finance and ethics filing duties, sign rules, a neutral FAQ. Staff and city resources must not be used to advocate for or against candidates or measures; confirm the state rule.
5. **Measure materials:** impartial analysis, if required, is written neutrally by the proper official; the city may inform but not advocate with public funds (verify state law).
6. **Transition:** orientation, oath of office, committee reassignments, and vacancy filling for unexpired terms.
7. **Public information plan:** deadlines, polling places, and accessibility and language-assistance obligations.

## Output structure
- Master calendar table: date | milestone | legal source (to be verified) | owner | status
- Council agenda calendar for required actions
- Risk list and contingencies (late measure, recount, vacancy)
- Communications schedule with neutrality guardrails

## Red flags / escalate to the city attorney
- Any use of city funds, staff time, or email lists related to a candidate or ballot measure
- Disputes over candidate eligibility, petitions, or ballot language
- Conflict between charter dates and state election code dates
- Council members acting as candidates and also voting on election administration

## Common mistakes
- Treating last year's dates as this year's; holiday and weekend shifts change deadlines
- Missing the filing deadline for a measure to reach the county
- Mixing informational outreach with advocacy language

*Verify every date with the election official and city attorney. Not legal advice.*
