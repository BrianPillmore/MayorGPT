## Purpose
Prevent structural deficits created by funding permanent costs with temporary money. Every ask must show its funding source, its duration, and what happens in year three.

## Required inputs
- Proposal (positions, program, contract, equipment, capital project) with first-year and full-year cost.
- Funding source and its duration (fund balance, grant, ARPA-type allocation, sale proceeds, settlement, one-time state aid, new recurring revenue).
- Current multi-year forecast and fund balance policy.

## Workflow
1. Classify the cost: one-time (equipment purchase, study, capital), recurring (salary, benefits, maintenance, licenses, debt service), or hybrid (a one-time purchase that creates recurring O&M).
2. Classify the revenue likewise. Treat grant, reimbursement, asset sale, settlement, fund balance drawdown, and one-time state allocations as one-time unless the agreement guarantees renewal.
3. Annualize: a mid-year hire costs more in year two. Add benefits, pension, training, equipment, vehicle, space, and supervision. Include escalators and renewals for contracts and software.
4. Build a three-to-five-year view: cost and funding by year, with the year the money runs out (the cliff).
5. Test fit: does the request match the revenue type? One-time money for one-time uses (capital, reserves, pension prepayment, debt payoff) is acceptable; one-time money for ongoing positions needs a documented sunset or a plan to absorb the cost.
6. Absorption plan: identify the offset (reduction, fee revenue, reprioritization) that will fund the recurring cost when the one-time money ends, and the decision-maker who owns it.
7. Recommendation: approve as proposed, approve with sunset/term-limited positions, restructure, or decline.

## Output structure
| Item | Type | Y1 | Y2 | Y3 | Y4 | Y5 | Funding source & duration | Cliff year | Absorption plan |

Add: recurring balance summary (recurring revenue minus recurring cost, before and after the proposal) and draft council language for conditions (sunset, report-back).

## Red flags / escalate
- Positions or contracts with no funding after year two.
- Fund balance used for recurring payroll.
- Grant-funded positions with no commitment to continue or terminate.
- Sale of assets to close an operating gap.
- Budget amendments that add recurring cost without a recurring source.

## Common mistakes
- Listing only year-one cost.
- Assuming grants renew.
- Ignoring benefit and pension loading.
- Treating a transfer between funds as new money.

## State-law variance
Balanced-budget definitions and permitted use of fund balance differ by state and charter.
