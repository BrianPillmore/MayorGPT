## Purpose
Convert an auditor's finding (financial audit, Single Audit, management letter, state auditor report, internal audit) into a management response that is specific, owned, dated, and verifiable. Auditors judge responses on whether the root cause is addressed, not on tone.

## Required inputs
- Finding text verbatim: condition, criteria, cause, effect, and recommendation (the five elements).
- Finding classification if stated: material weakness, significant deficiency, compliance finding, questioned costs, or management-letter comment.
- Program/fund affected, fiscal year, dollar amount questioned, and whether it repeats a prior-year finding.
- Response deadline and who receives it (council audit committee, state auditor, federal cognizant agency, pass-through entity).

## Workflow
1. Restate the five elements in one paragraph each. If the auditor omitted cause, say so and draft the cause hypothesis for staff to confirm.
2. Decide the stance: agree, partially agree, or disagree. Disagreement requires evidence (documents, statute, policy) and should be reviewed by the city attorney. Never concede a legal conclusion in a response without counsel review.
3. Find the root cause: missing policy, policy not followed, staffing/segregation-of-duties gap, system limitation, training gap, or vendor/subrecipient failure. A training fix for a system problem will recur.
4. Draft corrective actions as a table: action, owner by title, start date, completion date, evidence of completion (reconciliation, signed policy, system report), and interim compensating control.
5. For federal awards, check whether the finding implicates questioned costs, repayment, a corrective action plan under 2 CFR 200.511, or the pass-through entity's management decision timeline. Confirm current thresholds and deadlines.
6. For repeat findings, explain why the prior corrective action failed. Auditors and councils treat unexplained repeats as a control-environment issue.
7. Identify cost: new software, FTE, or contracted help. Label as one-time or recurring and name the funding source or the budget amendment needed.
8. Draft the response in plain language, 150 words or fewer per finding, plus the action table. Add a status-tracking cadence (quarterly report to the audit committee or finance committee).

## Output structure
| Finding | Classification | Stance | Root cause | Corrective action | Owner | Due | Evidence of completion | Cost (one-time/recurring) |

Then: management response text, interim controls, follow-up testing plan, and items needing legal or council action.

## Red flags / escalate
- Material weakness, suspected fraud, or misappropriation: stop and involve the city attorney, the audit committee chair, and (where state law requires) the state auditor or law enforcement.
- Questioned costs or repayment exposure on federal or state awards.
- Repeat finding for a second or third year.
- Finding that implicates the finance director, city manager, or an elected official: route outside the normal chain.
- Debt covenant or continuing-disclosure impact (late or qualified audit).

## Common mistakes
- Promising "will review procedures" without owner, date, or evidence.
- Setting a completion date before the next audit fieldwork that cannot be met.
- Blaming staff turnover as the cause without a fix for the control gap.
- Over-committing to new staff or software without budget authority.
- Agreeing with a finding's legal characterization without counsel review.

## State-law variance
Audit committee requirements, state auditor filing deadlines, and public-release timing differ by state. Confirm local rules before publishing a response.
