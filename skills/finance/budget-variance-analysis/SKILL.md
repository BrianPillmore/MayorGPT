description: Explain monthly or quarterly variances by separating timing noise, structural variance, one-time events, and management action needs.
argument-hint: [fund or department]
---

## Purpose
Produce a fund-level variance explanation that tells the manager and council which variances are timing, which are structural, and what action (if any) is needed before year end. Municipal budgets are modified-accrual, fund-based, and seasonal, so straight-line "percent of year elapsed" comparisons mislead.

## Required inputs
- Adopted budget, amended budget (with amendment log), YTD actuals, encumbrances, and prior-year same-period actuals, by fund and department.
- Revenue seasonality: property tax installment dates, sales tax remittance lag (commonly one to two months), state shared revenue schedules, grant reimbursement timing.
- Known one-time events: settlements, storm response, insurance proceeds, asset sales, large purchase orders.
- Basis of accounting used in the report (cash, modified accrual) and whether encumbrances are included.

## Workflow
1. Confirm the comparison basis. Use a seasonally adjusted budget (or prior-year same-period share) instead of straight-line. State which you used.
2. Separate revenue from expenditure variance by fund. Never net across funds, and never net restricted against unrestricted money.
3. Classify each variance above the materiality threshold (for example, the greater of a set dollar amount or a percentage of the line; use the city's policy if one exists):
 - Timing: reimbursements, billing cycles, invoices not yet posted, encumbered but not spent.
 - Structural: rate, volume, or price change that will persist (wage settlement, utility rates, declining collections, contract escalator).
 - One-time: non-recurring event. Say whether it was budgeted.
 - Error or coding: misposting, wrong fund, accrual miss.
4. Project year-end by line using the classification, not a run-rate. Provide low, expected, and high cases.
5. Check personnel lines: vacancy savings (recurring or temporary?), overtime vs. vacancies, and unfunded step or COLA obligations.
6. Check restrictions: enterprise funds covering general fund costs, interfund loans, grants drawn but not reimbursed, special-revenue fund deficits.
7. State actions: no action, monitor, administrative transfer within authority, budget amendment requiring council, or hiring/spending freeze. Identify the legal appropriation level (fund, department, or object) because it determines whether council action is needed.

## Output structure
| Fund/Dept | Line | Budget (YTD adj.) | Actual | Variance $ / % | Type (timing/structural/one-time/error) | Year-end projection (L/E/H) | Action |

Add: three-sentence headline, structural-variance summary with recurring annualized effect, amendments requested, and a list of questions for department heads.

## Red flags / escalate
- Projected overspend above the legal appropriation level (possible statutory violation in many states).
- Negative cash in any fund or an interfund loan not authorized by council.
- Revenue shortfall that is structural and exceeds the contingency or reserve policy.
- Unusual journal entries near period end, or large unreconciled differences.
- Grant-funded costs spent ahead of an executed agreement.

## Common mistakes
- Annualizing a seasonal month.
- Treating vacancy savings as permanent while hiring is planned.
- Ignoring encumbrances, so a "underspent" line is already committed.
- Using one-time savings to cover a structural gap.
- Reporting percentages without dollars on small lines.

## State-law variance
Appropriation level, amendment procedures, and mid-year reporting duties vary by state and charter.
